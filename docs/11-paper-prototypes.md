# FIRST SLICE — Paper Prototypes (Phase 0)

**Goal:** Test the two most important feelings in the game *before* any code or greybox art.  
If these two interactions already feel warm and clear on paper, the vertical slice has a real foundation.

---

## Prototype 1 — Serving Conversation (Order Card Game)

### What we are testing
Serving is a **conversation**, not a stress puzzle.  
Can a new player feel competent, taught, and kind — without timers, fail states, or jargon walls?

### Materials needed
- Index cards or sticky notes
- 3–5 “customer” cards
- 1 “player” role
- Optional: a simple counter surface (table edge)

### Customer Cards (use these exact ones for the first test)

**Card 1 – Regular Coffee**  
Order text: “Regular coffee.”  
Teach moment: If player hesitates or asks → “Milk and two sugars. That’s what regular means here.”  
Success feeling: Player learns something useful without being corrected.

**Card 2 – Bacon Egg and Cheese**  
Order text: “Bacon egg and cheese. Salt, pepper, ketchup.”  
Teach moment: “That whole sentence is a complete order. You’re speaking New York now.”  
Success feeling: Player feels a small spark of belonging.

**Card 3 – The Quiet Regular (Mr. Idris)**  
Order text: “The usual.”  
Teach moment: Rosa (or facilitator) quietly says: “Two sugars. Every day for nine years. You’ll see him tomorrow.”  
Success feeling: Player feels the weight of being trusted with someone’s routine.

**Card 4 – Uncertain Customer (the kid)**  
Order text: “Whatever’s cold… I don’t really know.”  
Player choices:
- Just make something and hand it over
- Ask what they usually like
- Say “Chef’s welcome — I’ll pick for you”  
Success feeling: No choice feels wrong. Kindness is always available.

**Card 5 – Chopped Cheese (optional stretch)**  
Order text: “Chopped cheese on a hero, letta, tomahta, hots.”  
If player looks lost → customer smiles and explains one piece only. Never all at once.

### Player Choices (always available)
For every order the player may:
1. Say nothing and just make it
2. Repeat the order back (tiny memory moment)
3. Ask one question
4. Say “Chef’s welcome”

### Facilitation Script (5–7 minutes)
1. “You’re behind the counter. A customer walks up. There are no timers and you cannot fail.”
2. Read the order card.
3. Let the player respond naturally.
4. Only teach if they ask or clearly hesitate.
5. After 3–5 customers, ask:
   - Did any moment feel like a test?
   - Did you feel welcomed or corrected?
   - Which order do you still remember?

### Success Criteria
- Zero participants describe it as a “minigame” or “puzzle”
- At least 4/5 say they felt the city was being kind
- The “usual” moment lands emotionally for most people
- People can repeat at least one thing they learned

## Prototype 2 — Map Companion (Paper Fold & Write)

### What we are testing
Map is new here too.  
Does the paper object feel like a patient friend rather than a tutorial menu or quest log?

### Materials needed
- One real piece of paper, folded once (like a small zine or map)
- Pen or pencil
- The 12 Map notes from Doc 08 (printed or written on separate slips)

### Setup
- Page 1 (inside left): Almost blank. Only a small handwritten star and the words:  
  *“You are here. That’s enough.”*
- Page 2 (inside right): Empty margins labeled lightly in pencil: “Things I noticed” / “Words” / “Stories”
- Exterior can stay blank or have a simple soft cover.

### Test Flow (8–10 minutes)
1. Hand the folded Map to the participant at the start. Say nothing about it.
2. Play through the serving prototype or simply narrate the Day 1 beats.
3. When a teachable moment happens, pause and say:  
   “Map wants to write this down. Want to see?”
4. If they open it, hand them the matching note slip or write it slowly in front of them in ordinary handwriting.
5. Let them fold it back themselves.
6. At the end ask:
   - Did Map feel like a system or like a companion?
   - Did you ever feel tested or behind?
   - Would you open this again tomorrow?

### Critical Rules During the Test
- Map never opens itself more than once unsolicited.
- Map never blocks progress.
- Map is allowed to be a little messy or imperfect.
- “Tell me” / curiosity is always met with delight, never a lecture.

### Success Criteria
- Participants describe Map with words like “friend,” “notebook,” “patient,” or “kind” — not “menu,” “log,” or “tutorial”
- At least half voluntarily open Map more than once
- No one feels they are falling behind by not opening it
- The first note (“You are here. That’s enough.”) lands as comforting

## Combined Mini Playtest (Recommended)

Run both prototypes in one 15-minute session with 5 people:

1. Minutes 0–2: Give Map, read the arrival beat
2. Minutes 2–9: Serving conversation (3–5 customers)
3. Minutes 9–12: Fire-escape / gold window narration + final postcard moment
4. Minutes 12–15: Open questions

Record exact phrases people use. Those phrases become future marketing and UI copy.
