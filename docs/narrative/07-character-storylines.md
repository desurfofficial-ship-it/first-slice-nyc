## 1. How to read

Each arc lists: **throughline** (what the character wants across all chapters), **chapter beats** (only the beats that are *theirs* — Leo-dependent beats live in doc 06), **own decisions & consequences**, and **without Leo** (the default trajectory if the player never engages them). Supporting-cast behavior notes are §6; the simulation tick spec is §7.

## 2. Major arcs

### 2.1 Eddie Carrasco
**Throughline:** pass the shop whole; see his son without surrender.
**Beats.** C0: hires Leo on instinct; administers the ritual. C1: the framed notice; "promises are flour." C2: the renewal he didn't earn honestly — his relief is total and his eyes aren't ("Who did you promise, and what did it cost them?"). C3: the heart attack (Jul 6); the keys; rehab at Junior's; the pen for the signing. C4: three days a week behind the counter; watches the Face happen with a mechanic's suspicion; asks one question about the Club ("Whose money?" — and accepts the answer he knows is partial). C5: Charlotte; the last night happens without him and he knows it; the phone call ("Both true."). C6: the floor in Charlotte, offered twice; his health holds if Leo's amends run honest — canon does not kill Eddie in the main window.
**Own decisions.** Keeps the slice ritual until the end; never tells Leo about the Gino-era favor he himself accepted in Y-30 (the DOB ghost is older than Leo thinks — doc 08 §4.1).
**Without Leo.** The lease war goes worse: Eddie loses the shop in C3 instead, sells the name to a midtown operator, and spends C4–C6 at Junior's, alive and diminished; the ritual dies with the corner. The block's C5 is colder for it.

### 2.2 Rosa Delgado
**Throughline:** keep the block working; be load-bearing, not "nice."
**Beats.** C0–C1: the teaching. C2: the anniversary toast; the withdrawal of warmth as *method*. C3: lends the cigar box's history (Y-11, the down payment she owes Doris for); tells Leo to ask Eleanor about the rain. C4: sees the Face and prices it; feeds him anyway — hospitality as judgment, her sharpest tool. C5: the terminal line. C6: the cigar box's last page; she redeems exactly one IOU ever — the player learns whose in the epilogue window (canon: Deshaun's, for the ambulance that came for Eddie).
**Own decisions.** Refuses the Club (doc 09 §4); refuses Corvus's naming-rights offer for the billboard in C4 (two sentences, no speech).
**Without Leo.** Rosa's arc is nearly Leo-independent by design: the teaching happens with Sam, or with the next stranger. The block's memory-keeper doesn't need a protagonist; she needs the light on. Her only Leo-dependent branch is the C5 terminal line, which fires at whoever walks up the stairs broken — in the ignore-spine world, it's Yoli she says it to.

### 2.3 Yolanda "Yoli" Ferreira
**Throughline:** a land trust before the crane; don't become Ernie.
**Beats.** C1: the freezer drive; the uncle line. C2: builds the Compact; is broken by it *with* Leo's hand; takes the Oktays' loss as tuition — hers, priced in people (her own C2 mistake, per doc 05 §2.3: all-or-nothing strategy). C3: rebuilds smaller: the garden, the hearing testimony, the paperwork of resistance. C4: takes Kev in; starts the title search that ends in El Vann's paper wall. C5: the coalition's darkest hour — the receipts she hated become the leverage she needs; refuses to use Leo's fall as a campaign prop (her line: "We're not going to eat him. We're going to out-organize him."). **Runs for council in C6** against Reyes's anointed successor: loses, 38–62, and wins the thing she actually wanted — the Compact papers enter the record, Corvus concedes 220→280 affordable units and a community land trust option on the garden slot (the C6 coalition win is *hers*; Leo's testimony is optional seasoning, doc 09 §8).
**Own decisions.** The C2 strategy; refusing Corvus's liaison salary (the letter in the desk); the C6 run itself.
**Without Leo.** The Compact dies in C2 of Craig's refusal; Yoli loses the Oktays anyway (slower, less publicly), hardens earlier, starts the title search a year sooner out of pure spite, and **wins the C6 hearing by a wider margin**. The game is honest: Leo's best-case intervention changes the *speed* of her win, never its possibility.

### 2.4 Kevin Okafor
**Throughline:** matter somewhere real; get Grace's name off the past-due column.
**Beats.** C0–C1: weekend counter, the lanyard (F6's trigger). C2: full-time; the college denial; $200/mo to Grace. C3: Leo's protégé — doors open; he confuses access with belonging. C4: the pledge video; the audit; the meme; JMA's hard line; the screenshots folder grows. C5: the receipts handover (his decision, argued with himself for six weeks — Deniz didn't recruit him; *he* offered); the aftermath: famous for the worst reason in his life. C6: the amends path — if Leo completes the hardest amends (the reference letter to the union apprenticeship, signed in person, no camera), Kev takes it; if not, he's still at Halcyon's bar in C6, telling the story wrong. Canon keeps him alive, employed, and un-corrected by the narrative: his resentment is *rational*.
**Own decisions.** The screenshots habit itself; the handover; refusing Leo's first apology in C5 ("Don't be sorry at me. Be sorry at my mother.").
**Without Leo.** Kev stays a counter kid, saves for a certified course, gets it in C5 on his own, and is fine — the version of his life the Club's collapse interrupted. The block's Kev-shaped hole in the ignore-spine world is smaller than anyone expects, which is its own comment.

### 2.5 June Park
**Throughline:** hers on paper and in practice; honesty as floor.
**Beats.** C1: the DOB wall; the spark. C2: opening; the lease with the same paperstock as Eddie's. C3: the romance deepens; the build-out loan strain; she catches him mid-performance once and names it kindly ("You do a voice when you're scared. It's a good voice. It's not yours."). C4: **the diary discovery** — her conditions, issued once, non-negotiable, take-it-or-leave: (1) Dr. Feld, actually attended, for a year; (2) the condition named out loud to her, not to the notebook; (3) the Club's books, shown, before she asks. If all three are met *and* he takes the C5 public reckoning (doc 06 N5c), C6 allows a slow restart — she hires him for one shift a week at Halcyon, which in her language is a betrothal. If any is missed: she's in C6 as the block's success story whose window light he knows not to look at. Both outcomes are canon-clean; neither is a reward.
**Own decisions.** The conditions themselves; refusing Leo's money for the lease fight in C5; telling Fitz the truth about the envelope for her brother.
**Without Leo.** Halcyon succeeds on schedule; her brother's envelope gets used in C4 for a rehab deposit; June's C6 is a person at peace, annoyed only at the timeline, not at anyone in it.

### 2.6 Marisol Quintero
**Throughline:** Juniper Commons done right, judged kindly by history.
**Beats.** C2: the coffee that isn't a pitch. C3: the offer. C4: the accomplice of convenience; the deck she knows is deck-shaped. C5: the cascade as *her* crisis too (construction debt doesn't wait for funerals); she testifies accurately, protects Wexler precisely as far as the letter requires, and never once lies on the record — her armor is technical truth. C6: the late offer to Leo (doc 09 §7): "You were the best community face I ever hired. That's not an apology. Come back when you want to be good at this again." The test: accepting re-runs the villain exam with better production values.
**Own decisions.** The Wexler method's plausible deniability; the 20% AMI line she fights the city up to but not past.
**Without Leo.** Corvus hires Grant Mercer (§8); the project is six months slower, 60 units stingier, and she wins everything anyway. The conflict was never dependent on the protagonist — that is the brief's spine and this doc's spine alike.

### 2.7 Dane Wexler
**Beats.** Functional at every stage; in C5 his procedural armor meets the Compact papers (title problem, three parcels) and he negotiates the concessions with genuine professional grace — the antagonist-adjacent man's arc is *competence being used for settlement instead of assembly*. C6: the partnership, the boat, the irony intact. **Without Leo:** identical minus one headache.

### 2.8 Gino Trabucco
**Beats.** C3: the lunch; the disappeared violation. C4: Craig's paper at 60 cents ("helping a neighbor"). C5: never on-screen for the cascade; the investigation reaches him as a *question*, not a charge ("Mr. Trabucco, did you discuss the plaintiff's permits?" — "I discussed sandwiches. I'm a generous man."). C6: sends a fruit basket to the diner Leo works at. No note. **Without Leo:** identical; he was here before and will be after; the story's point about old arrangements is that they don't need you to persist.

### 2.9 Craig Hollis
**Throughline:** one respectable win, at any rate.
**Beats.** C1: the charming squeeze. C2: the re-rating wave; the quiet drink after. C3: the books fight with Doris; the drinking deepens. C4: SaniHaul's paper; the schedule-riding Priya notices. C5: **sells the row** — the chapter's structural act; at the funeral he is the man everyone is kind to and no one sits with; after the sale he is briefly, horribly *relieved*, and the game lets the player see it. C6: Fort Lauderdale again, managing a marina; sends the estate's last friend-price ledger to Rosa with a sticky note: "She kept this. I couldn't." The block's verdict on him is complicated forever — the bible refuses the flat villain read.
**Own decisions.** All of it, freely; the paper didn't sign itself.
**Without Leo.** He sells in C2, faster and sloppier; Doris lives to see it, which is worse for everyone, especially him.

### 2.10 Doris Hollis
**Beats.** C1–C2: the sabbatical that isn't; the ledger she keeps in her head. C3: back; sharp; tells Leo at the door, true-thing-last: "You're a good boy the way Craig's a good boy. Watch it." C4: the boiler math; the El Vann letter's existence hinted to Yoli ("Ask Eleanor. Then ask your father why she'd keep it."). C5: **dies Sept 14** — the conflict's hinge, on her own clock, unrelated to Leo (the City-Moves Law's heaviest demonstration). **Without Leo:** identical; she was always going to die in this window; the difference is whether anyone was kind to Craig at the funeral.

### 2.11 Priya Nair
**Throughline:** every station; be the system's memory, not its hand.
**Beats.** C0: the wrong-train rescue. C1–C3: the intel economy (trades, never gifts — the "Subway University" walks expand through the chapters; her station-stamp album is a quiet running joke that lands hard in C5 when the album's last page is the Works station, which doesn't exist). C4: watches Craig's schedule-riding and files it under *not mine to say*. C5: **the handover** — the ledger she sat on; the pricing of neutrality; her own C2-mistake-shaped reckoning (doc 05 §2.11). C6: gives the station-stamp album to Eli ("Start late. Finish anyway." — her only sentimental act, and she'd deny it). **Without Leo:** the handover happens anyway (Reyes's need is independent); her arc is the cleanest proof that the fall's machinery doesn't require the protagonist's presence.

### 2.12 Deshaun Carter
**Throughline:** the patch; be the one who knows the protocol.
**Beats.** C0: the platform save. C1–C2: the friendship; the study group that is mostly him studying near Leo. C3: **fails the paramedic exam** — his own chapter, unconnected to any plot event; the scene where Leo finds him on the fire escape and doesn't manage him, just sits there, is the friendship's real foundation. C4: passes it on the retake; the pinning ceremony is the block's best party of the year and the Face does *not* speak at it (Leo offers; Deshaun: "Nah. You'd make it a keynote."). C5: Verna's $2,300 — he doesn't abandon Leo; he *invoices* him: six months of no contact, then a diner booth, then "Start talking, and don't do the voice." C6: the cousin's cousin job; the amends ledger's most forgiving creditor. **Without Leo:** passes the exam the first time in C3 (the retake delay is the one Leo-shaped dent in his arc); Verna never invests; his life is better and he'd never once say so.

### 2.13 Mr. Idris Warsame
**Beats.** The 6:55 ritual through C0–C4; the $600; refuses to speak at the C5 garden reckoning and thereby speaks loudest; C6: the fajr walk continues; the amends payment he accepts **only** if it funds Amina's textbook bill directly, from Leo to the bursar, never through his hands. **Without Leo:** identical minus the wound; his granddaughter's tuition is tight either way, which is the block's truth.

### 2.14 Deniz Oktay
**Throughline:** the record; the license; the dignity.
**Beats.** C2: the displacement; the sign-in sheet photographed. C3: Halcyon's bar; cold civility. C4: annotates; waits; her discipline is the story's scariest virtue. C5: **publishes** — and her scene with Leo (doc 06) is the fall's moral center: she is not cruel; she is *bookkeeping*. C6: sits the CPA exam; the amends she accepts is not money — it's the deposition: Leo's sworn statement of the C2 meeting, for the coalition's record, on the record, forever. **Without Leo:** the Compact still dies; the store still dies; she publishes *Craig's* paper trail instead — a smaller, colder story, and she wins it smaller and colder.

### 2.15 Rachel Voss
**Throughline:** her own shop; one friendship per year that survives the year.
**Beats.** C4: the gallery; the circling; the payphone mention (once, flat). C5: the 1 a.m. call ("You did this in Ohio too."). C6: **friendship only, and only after the amends ledger shows movement** — she is the one person the bible forbids the reconciliation-romance default with: if the player has done the work, the game offers a *friendship scene* of real warmth (the metal-shop visit, her teaching him to weld badly) and leaves the rest open, unforced, unpunished either way. Her non-negotiable: she will never again be anyone's self-improvement project, including his.
**Own decisions.** The move to New York; the unopened letter from Y-2 that she finally returns in C6, answered in three lines, the first of which is "I got your postcards."
**Without Leo.** She never appears; her arc happens entirely off-screen and is *fine*, which the epilogue's city-diary mentions once, gently ("The welder from Inwood got her shop. The block heard. The block approves.").

### 2.16 Camille Odom
**Beats.** C3: the pantry; the alternative life made specific (a Tuesday that repeats). C4: the exit on her terms. C6: if the player's post-fall path includes the ordinary-life emphasis for a full in-game year, one letter arrives — not a reconciliation; a *reference*: the pantry needs a logistics volunteer with his exact skills. The choice of whether to show up is the player's entire relationship with her thereafter. **Without Leo:** the pantry gets its funding in C5 from a Reyes earmark; she never transfers; the Bronx school line belongs only to the romance path.

## 3. The ensemble web (who's connected to whom, and how information moves)

Load-bearing edges (each with its information type): Eddie↔Rosa (37 years of trade credit and truth), Tito↔Doris (the 1994 silence — both saw the vote), Yoli↔Tito (Sunday dinner; the uncle-shaped hole), Kev↔Grace (the money), Deniz↔June (employer; the receipts live at Halcyon's back office from C3), Deshaun↔Darnell (courts), Priya↔everyone (the 7:40 platform is the wire), El Vann↔Yoli (the papers), Father Mike↔Gino (the parking lot lease — the church's one compromised edge, resolved in C6 when Father Mike tears it up after the concessions), Idris↔Imam Dawan (the fajr walk), Marisol↔Wexler (the method), Wexler↔Gino (the stipend), Craig↔SaniHaul (the paper), Ada↔Leo (the envelope economy).

**Gossip physics recap (doc 04 §4):** public acts are editorial by evening, front-page by Sunday; private acts stay private as long as no one is hurt enough to talk. Implementable rule: every character has a *disclosure threshold* (doc 14 R5) — the hurt that makes them talk.

## 4. The dominoes senate

Corner Park's table is the neighborhood's deliberative body and the game's Greek chorus. Tito presides; Eddie loses on purpose; the 1994 story is the senate's suppressed minute. Three canon senate scenes per chapter (C1: "who is the new boy"; C3: "whose block is the render on"; C5: "what do we do with him now"). The senate never plot-drives; it *prices* — after every senate scene, the block's behavior toward Leo shifts in small legible ways (a wave, a price held, a stool kept, a second half-slice).

## 7. Off-screen simulation spec (the tick)

Each major has a calendar the sim advances even unvisited: income events, appointments, moods (three-state: steady / strained / bitter — with recovery paths), and one *independent decision per chapter* (already listed in this doc's beats). Requirements: R5 (doc 14). The tick is honest about scope: majors simulate decisions and availability; supporting cast simulate *presence and news* only. Nobody off-screen gets a personality transplant to serve a beat.

## 8. The world without you

If the spine is ignored: Grant Mercer, 36 — Corvus's professional engagement lead ("I hear the block, I really do") — becomes the project's face in C3. The block gets a worse Leo: no soup in the blackout, no ritual, a QR code where the flyercard was. The comparison is never stated by the game; the city's diary makes it once: *"The new one smiles at everybody. The block remembers soup."* The fall, meanwhile, happens to *nobody* — Mercer rises, Yoli wins slower, Kev is fine, and the player's Leo is a man who kept his head down and his hands clean and his life small: a valid ending-shaped life the bible refuses to punish, and refuses to call a story. The city's diary's last word on that path: *"Some people come to New York and just... live here. It's allowed. It's allowed."*
