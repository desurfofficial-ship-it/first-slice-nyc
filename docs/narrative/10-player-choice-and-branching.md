## 1. The choice taxonomy

| Class | Definition | Examples | Mechanical home |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Snapshot** | In-scene dialogue/behavior choices with local effect | The anniversary toast; how Leo handles Idris's questions; the cart mishear's follow-up | Scene state |
| **Structural** | Chapter-scale nodes that set relationships, access, and the fall's shape | N2's three forms; the investor list; the El Vann papers; the public reckoning | World flags + ledger |
| **Silent** | Omissions — the thing he said he'd do and didn't | The flyers delivered late; the board meeting skipped; the lawyer called late; the diary entry not written | Omission tracker |
| **Time-boxed** | Opportunities with windows that close and do not reopen | The warming-station volunteer sign-ups; the jobs-fair speaker slot; Yoli's C2 strategy session; the gallery night | Scheduler (doc 14 R6) |

**The omission tracker is the bible's quiet innovation and its most important system.** Leo makes promises. The world remembers promises independently of cutscenes: Eddie's "promises are flour" is a *system*, not a line. Every promise is a flag with a due date; unmet promises mature into the same ledger as betrayals — smaller coin, same currency. A player who never breaks a promise gets a different fall (smaller, lonelier — he owes nobody and is owed nothing); a player who breaks them all gets the cascade with extra teeth. Doc 14 R2 specifies the storage.

## 2. Rails vs free space (the honest map)

**Rails (unskippable, shapeable):** R1 arrival texture · R2 Betrayal I fires (form chosen) · R3 the Face ascent fires (depth chosen) · R4 the villain-question fires (content assembled from ledger) · R5 the fall fires (extent chosen) · R6 continuation stance free.

**Free space (never gated):** friendships, romances, side work, the court, the garden, the subway, postcards, the fluency ladder, the diary's voice, where to live in the aftermath, whom to speak to ever again.

**The design's promise to the player, stated once, in-game, by Priya, in C1:** "The train's going to Brooklyn either way. You pick the seat." The rails are the train; the free space is the seat; the bible's job is that both are real.

**No fake choices rule:** a choice whose options converge on the same outcome is permitted only when the *meaning* genuinely doesn't differ (flavor nodes: which stall, which mug). Every structural node above diverges in ledger terms. If a build ships a structural node that converges, it is a canon bug (doc 15).

## 5. Different personal histories (the chapter-end record)

At each season cut, the diary generates a **personal-history page** in Leo's voice — three to six lines, assembled from the actual flags (not from prose templates keyed to a score): what he was, what he did, whom it cost, what he wrote about it. Across the whole game these pages compose the *other* autobiography — the one the player can reread. The self-portrait (doc 03 §7) is their final page. There is no global morality score anywhere in the pipeline; the history pages are prose because lives are prose.

## 6. Consequence visibility (the city tells you, eventually)

Every structural choice becomes public through the gossip physics (doc 04 §4) within one to three in-game weeks, by a *specific* channel (the senate, the wire services, the counter, the 7:40). The player is never punished invisibly: they may not know *when*, but they will know *that*, from *someone*, in *character*. Conversely, consequences never arrive through narrator voice-over or UI banners. If the build needs to tell the player something, it finds a neighbor to do it.

## 7. What the player cannot choose

The condition (doc 02); the arrival (R1); Doris's death; the fall's existence; other people's forgiveness; the city's attention. The bible's wager: a story with real rails and real room is more free than a story that pretends everything bends.
