**The six load-bearing rails** (what the player cannot skip, and what they always shape): **R1** the arrival texture is fixed (doc 11); **R2** Betrayal I happens — its *form* is chosen (doc 08 §3); **R3** the ascent into the Face happens — its *depth of compromise* is chosen; **R4** the villain-question scene fires — its *content* is assembled from the player's ledger; **R5** the fall fires — its *extent and who it burns* are shaped by the whole history; **R6** the continuation stance is free (doc 09 §5). Everything else is player space. A player who ignores the spine gets all independent events (doc 04 §7) and a harder, emptier fall — absence is itself a choice the ledger tracks (doc 10 §3).

**Time-skip interstitials:** chapter transitions are *season cuts* — a diary montage (doc 03 §6), one postcard, and a three-line world-state whisper from the city's diary. Skips are diegetic jumps of the in-game calendar (weeks, never real-world time), consistent with the day-based sim (doc 14 R4).

## C0 — THE FIRST SLICE

**Period.** Tuesday, Sept 9 – Monday, Sept 15, Y1. Seven days, golden hour.
**Starting circumstances.** Leo steps off the interstate coach at Alder Heights Terminal with $87, a duffel bag, a job listing on his phone, and a green notebook. He has never been to New York. The full beat-by-beat script is doc 11; this section logs the chapter's story logic.
**Important characters.** Rosa, Eddie, Mr. Idris, Eli (the kid, unseen then seen), Deshaun, Sam (Day 4 stranger), Priya (one platform scene).
**Central conflict.** Arrival vs. imagination: the city he studied on a screen keeps correcting the city in his shoes. Nothing dramatic is allowed to happen (doc 11 §9) — the conflict is the gap between his confidence and his competence.
**Events.** 1) The terminal, the first walk, the wrong train (recovered by Priya). 2) The bodega: Rosa's warm window, the mango errand, "You're new. Rosa trains 'em right." 3) Corner Slice, 6:40 a.m.: the trial shift; the first slice ritual ("Eat standing up. That's the job."). 4) The panic-attack-on-the-platform beat → Deshaun's kindness ("You're not dying. You're new. Same symptoms."). 5) Rosa's lunch rush favor: the ugly cart, the handover line — *"It's ugly. So was mine. It's yours."* Leo hears **give**. The diary's first over-tag. 6) The cart days: Idris's usual, the kid in the gold window, the fire escape at dusk, the first postcard. 7) Day 4: the stranger with the duffel — Rosa: *"You looked like that once. Go say hi."* Hospitality Law, shown not told. 8) Days 5–7: the block opens up (Sunrise Laundry, Sal; the vending-permit filing, the receipt on the cart — all shipped beats, recontextualized as the block teaching him its grammar).
**Important conversations.** Eddie & the ritual (intention: the shop as covenant, stated in six words); Rosa & the cart (intention: the mishear planted fair); Deshaun & the platform (intention: the first person who knows the city's rules and shares them free); Idris's usual (intention: what a regular is — "two sugars, every day, you'll see him tomorrow").
**Player opportunities.** Learn the block (map fills), the cart mini-venture, first postcards, the fluency ladder's first rung.
**Major decisions.** None structural. Micro-choices only (what to write first in the diary; whether to follow Rosa or roam — Roam is legal from minute one).
**Short-term consequences.** The cart is Leo's; the corner knows him; Kev is hired weekend counter.
**Long-term consequences.** The mishear (K1) is now in the record; Eddie has seen how he treats the mop; Rosa has seen how he treats a gift.
**Character development.** Stage I begins (doc 02 §7). The diary voice: performative, funny, listing everything.
**Diary/map developments.** The Map's first ink: Terminal, bodega, Corner Slice, the plaza, the fire-escape view. Entries: three PLACE, two PERSON (v1 cards), one EVENT (the ritual slice), one QUESTION ("Who empties the terminal bins at 3 a.m.?"). Hooks planted, all small, none criminal: the lease notice on Eddie's wall (the player may notice it before Leo does), the surveyor photographing the Works at dawn (doc 11 §8: mundane — sun angles and square feet), Miss Odessa's second job.
**Handoff to C1.** The listing becomes full-time; the cart goes back on loan-schedule; Eddie says, "Six a.m. Monday. Real shift. Leave the tricks in Ohio."

## C1 — THE WARM LINE

**Period.** Sept 16 – Dec 23, Y1. (Interstitial: the season cut compresses the first three weeks.)
**Starting circumstances.** Leo is the shop's full-time counter and semi-official fixer of small things. The block has begun to see him. Eddie's first lease renewal notice is framed on the shop wall like a threat.
**Important characters.** Eddie, Rosa, Kev, June (introduced at the Halcyon build-out wall), Yoli (the garden), Deshaun, Craig (first appearance — the "renegotiation" visit), Marisol (a rendering on a bus-shelter poster; no contact yet), Miss Odessa & Eli, Tito.
**Central conflict.** The neighborhood's warmth is real and so is the arithmetic underneath it: the first renewal notice says the friend-price era has a countdown. Leo's conflict: he promised Eddie he'd "handle the landlord," and he has no idea what handling costs.
**Events.** 1) The full-time shift; the Slice Ladder's first rung named out loud by Eddie ("Counter now. Maybe the register by spring. Don't ask me for the keys." — the keys motif begins). 2) The Halcyon build-out hits a DOB wall; Leo routes June to Priya's knowledge ("inspections are a schedule, not a mystery"); first taste of knowing-people-beats-knowing-things. 3) The garden: Leo helps Yoli's freezer drive — genuinely; she thanks him like an auditor. 4) **F1** fires (Sunrise Laundry, the dryer bank, end of day — doc 02 §6.2); three short entries; he tells no one. **F6** (Kev's delivery lanyard) the same week. 5) **The nor'easter (Oct 29–Nov 1).** Four days without power. Corner Slice becomes the block's warming station: gas oven, candle tables, soup, the K-Globes dark for the first time in anyone's memory. Leo works all four days; the block sees him. The city's diary notes it *once*, the morning after. 6) The flyer board's lost-cat slip is replaced by a petition slip (the rezoning rumor, first physical form). 7) Craig's "renegotiation" visit: charm, apology, a number with a 7 in it — walked back for now. 8) December: the renewal notice *without* the walk-back. Leo promises Eddie again. Eddie: "Promises are flour. Show me bread."
**Important conversations.** The blackout table (intention: the block at its best, and Leo learning that being seen feels like a drug); Craig at the counter (intention: the antagonist as mirror — broke, charming, certain); Rosa's cigar box (intention: the block's real economy is memory); Yoli on the Compact's history (intention: plant 1994 in one sentence: "Last time this block trusted a petition, my uncle was holding the pen." — she says it lightly. It is not light.)
**Player opportunities.** The warming-station nights (the block's gratitude, converted — unfairly — into Leo's capital); side work (catering help for Fatima, DJ-adjacent errands for Deshaun); the court; the garden.
**Major decisions.** **The promise.** Player chooses how far Leo goes to "handle" it: (a) honest talk with Eddie about odds, (b) quiet research, (c) the direct approach to Craig's office. None break anything yet. All three are logged.
**Short-term consequences.** Leo's standing: rising. Eddie's trust: rising, with one held-back element (the keys). June: a professional friendship with a spark.
**Long-term consequences.** The blackout makes Leo the shop's public face — the exact face C4 will sell. The pet leashed here (fixing by access, not by labor) is the muscle C2 will overtrain.
**Character development.** Stage I maturing: "I'm here to become somebody" starts sounding like a plan.
**Diary/map developments.** The Map gains the back streets; PERSON cards v1 for Yoli (dismissive — "community agitator, probably never worked a real job" — the player's own later cringe is a feature), Craig, June. QUESTION entries: "Who is Craig scared of?" (answer arrives C4), "Why does the Works fence have a new lock?" EVIDENCE: the renewal notice photographed. First RECALL fragments logged with the wobble style.
**Handoff to C2.** The season cut lands on New Year's Day: the notice framed on the wall, Eddie taping the frame's corner, Leo writing *this is the year I fix it* — an over-tag if there ever was one.

## C2 — THE COMPACT

**Period.** Jan 6 – Jun 30, Y2.
**Starting circumstances.** Craig's ask is now formal: $7,200/mo at renewal or the space markets. Corvus's assemblage map has reached the block's mailboxes. Yoli convenes the merchants: a **Compact** — eleven businesses pledge unified renewal, mutual legal support, and a public no-sell stance. Doris, hospitalized for observation in February, is "on sabbatical"; Craig signs nothing.
**Important characters.** Yoli, Eddie, Rosa, Craig, the Oktays, Deniz, Marisol (first meeting), Priya, Kev, June, Miss Odessa.
**Central conflict.** Eleven signatures versus one strategy: the Compact's leverage requires unanimity, and unanimity is a knife anyone can pull. Leo sits inside three pressures — Eddie's survival, Yoli's plan, and Marisol's quiet interest in a "reasonable local voice."
**Events.** 1) The Compact's founding meeting at the garden (the player may attend; the sign-in sheet will matter — doc 08 §3). 2) Leo's seduction in miniature: Marisol's coffee, no ask, just respect ("You're the one who ran the warming station. I read everything."). 3) Priya's intel, traded for a favor: Craig is lying about Doris's recovery; the estate is leveraged; Corvus has already toured three of the eleven buildings. 4) The squeeze: Craig offers Leo privately — sign a separate renewal, friend rate locked five years, "you didn't hear it from me." 5) **The decision node (doc 08 §3; doc 10 §4, node N2).** The rails require a break; the player chooses its form: (a) pull Corner Slice out quietly (canon default — the shop's renewal arrives with a ribbon on it), (b) pull out *and* deliver two more signatures (Kev's mother's cleaning clients among them — deeper betrayal, better terms), (c) refuse and absorb the cost (the lease war continues; Eddie's shop bleeds; the break happens later in different clothes — R2 fires anyway, e.g., Leo trades Yoli's strategy meeting schedule to Priya's boss for a city grant that saves the shop). 6) **The Oktays pay.** With the Compact broken, Craig flips the hardware store's unit to the assemblage; Kestrel Hardware's rent doubles; by May they are out. The leaving scene: boxes, the register's last sale (a toy truck — Eli's), Deniz photographing the sign-in sheet with Leo's signature on the withdrawal line. 7) Rosa's 25th anniversary (June): the whole block, one long table, and a toast Leo is seated *across* from, not beside. Rosa's line, warm as ever, precise as a scalpel: "Twenty-five years. The block kept everything. Almost everything." 8) Kev's community-college appeal is denied (independent event); his money pressure becomes chronic. 9) Leo's room is re-rated $1,450 in Craig's rent wave — he pays it and says nothing, which the diary records as generosity and the ledger records as silence purchased.
**Important conversations.** The garden meeting (intention: the plan that deserves to work); Marisol's coffee (intention: recognition between predators who insist they aren't); the Oktays' last Saturday (intention: the cost made human — Mehmet's hands on the register); Rosa's toast (intention: the block's verdict, delivered as love); Eddie with the renewal (intention: relief without ignorance — Eddie is old, not blind; his line: "Who did you promise, and what did it cost them?").
**Player opportunities.** The Compact arc's alternate roles (organizer, dissenter, mediator); Halcyon's opening-night help (June, romance deepens); the side ventures.
**Major decisions.** Node N2 (the break's form); the Oktay moment (help carry boxes / write the reference letter / stay at the shop — the third is the coward's option and the diary knows it); the anniversary toast (raise a glass to Rosa's way or to "the future of the block" — one of the two makes Rosa stop smiling for exactly two seconds).
**Short-term consequences.** Corner Slice: renewed, friend rate, five years — Eddie's relief is total and wrong-footed ("Who did you promise?"). The Compact: dead as strategy, alive as grudge. Yoli: stops answering his first text.
**Long-term consequences.** The Oktay receipts (EVIDENCE, dated, deniable) exist from this chapter. Deniz's cold arithmetic begins. The K2 entry is written in June: *"The Compact was going to fail anyway. I saved the shop. Net good."* — tagged FACT, wrongly, and the player can feel it.
**Character development.** Stage II: "I'm useful. Useful is safe." The gap between his diary and his deeds opens to one person's width — Deniz's.
**Diary/map developments.** Map: the assemblage's footprint sketched from rumor; Corvus's render poster transcribed with a GUESS tag. Ledger K1 fires (the cart truth) — the first strikethrough, early in the chapter, teaching the mechanic before the chapter's bigger contradiction. **F3** fires (the M121 at dusk, riding the loop home — the bus window, the green sign, vending-machine coffee; logged in the wobble style between the Compact's two halves). QUESTION: "What did Doris sign in '94?" (El Vann's name surfaces for the first time — at the anniversary, Rosa: "Ask Eleanor about the rain that came inside.")
**Handoff to C3.** June's lease arrives with the same paperstock as Eddie's. June laughs it off. Leo doesn't. The season cut: July heat, the 4th, the shop's busiest day — and Eddie's hand on the counter, not moving.

## C3 — THE BRIDGE

**Period.** Jul 1 – Dec 20, Y2.
**Starting circumstances.** Eddie is in the hospital (heart attack, July 6). The shop is running on Leo. The block has begun routing its problems through him. Corvus needs a beloved face for Juniper Commons. Everything has a price and everything is for sale at once.
**Important characters.** Eddie (hospitalized, then recovering at Junior's), Marisol, Wexler (first meeting), Gino Trabucco (first lunch), Doris (back, diminished, sharp as ever), Craig (drinking), El Vann (first meeting), Tito, June, Camille (enters), Kev (promoted), Priya, Deshaun (the paramedic exam fail — his own chapter).
**Central conflict.** The trust handover: the block gives Leo its weight (shop, keys, problems, money) exactly as Corvus gives him its ladder (the offer). The conflict is whether "the bridge" is a person or a tollbooth.
**Events.** 1) The shop weeks: Leo runs Corner Slice for five weeks. The keys land in his hand — Eddie's held-back element, given from a hospital bed. **F2** fires at the visiting-room window (doc 02 §6.2). 2) El Vann: Leo comes to borrow the community room; leaves with the first look at the paper wall — the Compact file, Hartwell's option, the CLT bylaws. She says: "Developments fail, dear. Documents don't. They wait." 3) The Slice Summit (Aug 23): Leo convenes merchants, Corvus, and the board in the shop after hours; the "Alder Accord" talking points; a local blog's photo goes up: *The Man Who Feeds Alder Heights.* 4) The offer (Dec 9): Marisol, the render room, one page: run **Provisions Hall**, the food hall, "your vendors, your block, our kitchen." The pitch is perfect because it is true — and because it makes him the project's face. 5) The **Juniper Club** forms (Sept–Dec): Leo raises **$61,500 from 19 investors** to fund the hall's community build-out and its local hires (full list, doc 09 §4). The pitch decks are honest; the risk pages are thin; the mother's **$8,000** arrives Oct 3 in an envelope with a phone call that asks zero questions. 6) **Gino's first lunch (Nov 14).** No agenda. Deli sandwiches. The old DOB violation on Corner Slice (Eddie's paperwork ghost, years stale) is "looked into." It disappears. Leo does not tell Eddie. The diary records the lunch as "networking" and does not record the violation at all — the first entry the player can feel *refusing* to be written. 7) Doris, out of the hospital, finds Craig's version of the books. The fight is off-screen; its sound is on-screen (a door, a porch, silence). 8) June's Halcyon opens (Sept); Camille's pantry drive (Oct) — the "alternative life" enters the frame; Tito's dominoes table adopts Leo for the season, which is how he hears, without asking, that Yoli has noticed the Club's pitch meetings look like the Compact's, mirrored.
**Important conversations.** Eddie's keys (intention: trust as burden transfer — "Don't lose them. Not the shop. Them."); Marisol's offer (intention: the Faust draft, unsigned at chapter's end); El Vann (intention: the past as ammunition that doesn't care who fires it); Gino's lunch (intention: menace as hospitality — "Your landlord's a good boy. Bad credit, but a good boy."); the Club's closing pitch at the garden (intention: the audience that believes him — Miss Odessa's envelope).
**Player opportunities.** Run the shop (a real stretch of counter gameplay); the Club (choose investors, set terms — the player signs the thin pages too); the Summit's program (who speaks, who doesn't); romance tracks (June forward, Camille enter, both optional, both real).
**Major decisions.** Node N3a: accept/defer/refuse the offer (rails: acceptance is steered — R3 — but *when* and *how publicly* is chosen; refusal paths re-run the offer in C4 with worse terms); Node N3b: the Club's investor list (who he asks is who he can hurt); Node N3c: tell Eddie about Gino's lunch or not (not telling is available, tempting, and cheap *now*).
**Short-term consequences.** Leo is manager-operator with profit share; the Club is funded; the shop survives its hardest summer. Eddie's cardiac rehab is real and slow.
**Long-term consequences.** Nineteen debts. One signature pending. A fixer's favor outstanding. The papers in El Vann's wall are now something Leo has *seen* — knowledge is a loaded object in this story.
**Character development.** Stage III: "I'm the bridge. Bridges get tolls." The jokes sharpen (doc 13 §3).
**Diary/map developments.** Map: Corvus's office (sketched from outside), the Works' interior (one peek through the fence), Gino's deli. PERSON cards upgrade: Marisol v1 ("the most dangerous person who will ever buy me lunch"), Wexler, Gino ("reads naval history; eats olives like punctuation"). EVIDENCE: the offer letter (photographed, unsigned). QUESTION: "What is Craig's paper?" F2 logged with the wobble style and *one sentence of feeling* — the most he's written all year.
**Handoff to C4.** The season cut: New Year's, the shop, the offer letter signed on the 14th with Eddie's pen (Eddie, back behind the counter three days a week: "Sign it. A man should sign his own ladder." — the kindest sentence in the chapter, and wrong).

## C4 — THE FACE

**Period.** Jan 6 – Jun 28, Y3.
**Starting circumstances.** Leo is the public face of Juniper Commons: press, panels, the borough paper's profile. Provisions Hall is real: build-out money, a vendor list, a hiring promise — "500 local jobs" goes in the pledge video because Marisol's deck says 340 and Leo says 500 "because round numbers fund." The Club's first repayment window opens in spring.
**Important characters.** Kev (the face's face), Marisol, Wexler, June, Camille, Rachel (enters), Yoli, Deniz (at Halcyon now), Doris, Craig, Gino, Dr. Feld (enters), Tito, Deshaun, Miss Odessa, Eli.
**Central conflict.** The performance is winning and the ledger is bleeding: every constituency needs a different Leo, and the diffs are coming due. The villain question stops being rhetorical.
**Events.** 1) The ascent: the profile, the panel, the "40 names" list; Eddie's shop gets its busiest winter — and its last quiet one. 2) **The jobs-fair op (Mar 8).** Kev fronts the hiring pledge at a press conference — his face on the banner, his voice on the clip, Leo's numbers in his mouth. The audit lands in April: the jobs are "projected, condition-dependent." The clip becomes a meme: the kid who lied on TV. Kev's face does it; Leo's math did it (doc 08 §4). 3) Kev, humiliated, finds JMA's hard line. Yoli takes him in — the coalition gains its angriest researcher. 4) Camille's exit (Apr 26, if pursued): "You talk about people like they're pieces on a board. I'm on your board." If never pursued, her distance lands as one cold pantry scene and a transfer notice. 5) **June finds the diary (May 9).** Not snooping — dusting. The `secret` entries (doc 03 §9): the condition, K2, Gino's lunch, the room she never knew existed in her own boyfriend's head. The fight is quiet, which is how her fights work: "You let me believe I knew you. That's the part I can't renew." (K3 fires.) 6) **Rachel (May).** Fitz's gallery thing. She recognizes him instantly; he performs; she declines the performance. Over the chapter they circle: her questions are surgical ("Do you actually like any of these people, or do you *administer* them?"). She mentions the payphone once, flatly, and refuses to elaborate. 7) **Tito's story (May 30).** The dominoes table, late, cheap wine. Tito, unprompted, tells 1994: Ernie, the 214 signatures, the "consulting position," the rain that came inside. "You know what he said at the end? *The block was going to lose anyway. I saved what I could.*" Tito looks at his tiles. "Sit with that, muchacho. I sat with it thirty years." 8) The **villain-question scene (mid-June)** — spec in doc 08 §6. Trigger assembly: K2 + K4 read side by side; Tito's story; Deniz's presence at Halcyon; June's absence. No speech. A man with a notebook, at 2 a.m., under the K-Globes. 9) **Therapy begins (Jun 3).** Dr. Feld. The first session is 60% silence and it is the best scene in the chapter. 10) **The first missed repayment (Jun 24).** Quiet, administrative, terrible: a withdrawal that doesn't clear. He covers Miss Odessa's $140 personally, in cash, and tells no one. The diary tells on him anyway (EVIDENCE: the withdrawal slip, photographed, then deleted — the deletion is itself logged; nothing in this system truly deletes).
**Important conversations.** The pledge-video debrief with Kev (intention: the betrayal staged as mentorship — Leo's line "Big numbers protect the little guys" is the chapter's thesis-in-falsehood); June's fight (intention: privacy as trust's currency); Rachel at the gallery (intention: the un-impressible mirror); Tito's story (intention: the mirror at full length); Dr. Feld's silence (intention: a room where he is not performing).
**Player opportunities.** The hall's vendor list (who gets stalls — real stakes, real favors); the press cycle (deflect, own, or redirect the jobs story); romance triage; the therapy thread (sessions are playable, quiet, and mandatory-ish after the scene — the game models showing up, not curing).
**Major decisions.** Node N4a: the jobs number (correct the deck / let it fly / leak it yourself and blame Wexler — the last is available and dark); N4b: June after the discovery (full disclosure with the condition / partial / move out — three doors, one keeps her, barely, on conditions doc 07 §2.5 specifies); N4c: what he does with the El Vann papers (returns them / copies them / gives Yoli the location — the chapter records the choice; C5 prices it).
**Short-term consequences.** Kev's radicalization; June's conditional distance; the Club's books visibly strained; the Face at peak visibility and peak hollowness.
**Long-term consequences.** Every piece of the fall is now on the table: the receipts exist, the paper trail exists, the enemies are organized, the money is thin, and the one person who might have told him the truth (June) is behind a condition wall. **F4** and **F7** surface here.
**Character development.** Stage IV peak and crack: "I am the future of this block" → the 2 a.m. scene asks the question the chapter never answers out loud.
**Diary/map developments.** The ledger's heaviest entries (K3; K4 written honestly as GUESS and kept). The Map gains: the render room, the gallery, Dr. Feld's block. RECALL: F4 (the parking structure), F7 (the railing) — both logged with the wobble style, both followed by the shortest entries in the book. QUESTION tray: "Who am I when the room clears?" — the first QUESTION the diary cannot route to anyone else.
**Handoff to C5.** The season cut: July heat. The Club's next window. Craig, drunk at the laundromat, telling Sal the buildings are "as good as sold." The Works' fence gets its second new lock.

## C5 — NOTHING HOLDS

**Period.** Jul 3 – Nov 26, Y3.
**Starting circumstances.** The machine is loaded. Every trigger documented in doc 08 §5 is live; only the sequence is in question. Leo is at maximum exposure with minimum margin.
**Important characters.** Everyone; this is the ensemble's chapter.
**Central conflict.** The cascade: five independent failures, each survivable alone, none independent in practice. The fall is not an event; it is a *season*.
**Events.** 1) **Doris dies (Sept 14).** At home, in the chair, the ledger on her lap. The funeral is the last time the block is one thing. 2) Craig inherits and moves fast: the row goes to market; SaniHaul's paper matures; Corvus's offer is suddenly "fair." 3) **The receipts (Sept 21).** Kev hands Deniz the screenshots — every promise, every text, "in case." She publishes with annotations: the sign-in sheet with Leo's withdrawal line, the "stall the Oktays till they take anything" text, the 500-jobs math. The post is titled *The Paper Trail of the Man Who Feeds Alder Heights.* 4) **The run (Sept 22–30).** Nineteen investors become a crowd. The Club's books do not survive daylight. The landlord of Leo's own room is now, technically, Corvus-adjacent. 5) **Priya's handover (Oct 8).** The council office needs its own distraction; the investigation needs its cooperative witness; Kayla Fournier asks Priya what she "knows about the Marsh situation." Priya gives them the Club ledger she has been sitting on since spring. Her neutrality, priced at last. 6) **The investigation and the deal.** Civil and administrative, not criminal-lead: misrepresentations in a private offering, books kept "informally," Gino's favors surfacing as questions Leo can't answer well. The outcome: a deferred-prosecution-style agreement, restitution of **$47,300** after settlements, and a bar from operating the hall's concession. No prison. The game is honest about this being the *system's* mercy, not the *story's*. 7) **The shop is lost (Sept 30 close; Oct 4 last night).** Craig sells the row; Eddie's lease dies with Doris's handshake economy; Eddie, post-cardiac, has already gone to Charlotte. The last-night scene: Leo closes alone, cuts the last slice of the last pie, sets it on the counter for nobody, turns off the lights, and stands in the dark for four seconds longer than the scene needs. 8) **The exits.** June's conditions (doc 07 §2.5) — met or not, she is gone from the daily map; Rachel takes his one 1 a.m. call ("You did this in Ohio too." — the sentence he never recovers from); Eddie's call from Charlotte: "You were the best hire I ever made. And the worst thing that happened to that shop. Both true. Come see me when you're done being sorry." 9) **The phone call home (Nov).** Ada, the $8,000, zero questions — and this time he answers the question she doesn't ask. It is the shortest scene in the game and the only one the whole cast would applaud. **F5** fires the same week (the coins leaving, the dial tone after — Rachel's C4 retelling completing the circuit; logged in the wobble style, one line, then nothing). 10) **The terminal (Nov 26, Thanksgiving eve).** The city eats; the diner glows; Leo comes up the subway stairs with a duffel bag. From the bodega doorway, Rosa, to no one and to everyone: "You looked like that once." The chapter's last diary line: *"Day 1 again."* 11) The Inwood basement: $1,212 in the account, stairs that announce guests, a diner job in Long Island City via Deshaun's cousin. The main story's end state.
**Important conversations.** The funeral porch (Doris's lawyer, the estate's arithmetic); Deniz at the post's aftermath (intention: not gloating — bookkeeping: "I didn't do this to you. I *filed* you."); Priya on the platform (intention: her pricing, owned: "I told you once — learn which tile you are. You never asked which tile *I* was."); Eddie's call (intention: love without absolution); Ada (intention: the unconditional thing that ruins you properly); Rosa at the terminal (intention: the Hospitality Law closing its loop).
**Player opportunities.** The fall is *playable*: each trigger week has player-visible off-ramps the character doesn't take (doc 09 §2); the player's earlier history decides which off-ramps were even built (amends made early soften Kev's hand; no Gino lunches remove trigger four; the El Vann choice from N4c decides whether the coalition has its leverage when it needs it).
