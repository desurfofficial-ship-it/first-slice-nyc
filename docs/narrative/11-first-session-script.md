## 1. Design goals for the first session

1. The player falls for the block before the drama begins (the old game's strength, kept).
2. The diary is learned by *doing*: the tutorial is Leo writing entries, not UI tooltips (No-Quiz Law).
3. The condition is disclosed to the player early, lightly, and in Leo's voice — no diagnosis dump, no cutscene.
4. Nothing criminal, conspiratorial, or ominous. The three hooks planted are small, civic, and deniable.
5. The session ends with curiosity about five things: the man, the past, the people, the block, the city beyond.

## 3. Diary beats of the session (the tutorial IS the diary)

- **Entry 1** (cold open): the player writes it.
- **Entry 2** (after Eddie's ritual): prompt — *"What kind of place gives away the first slice?"* Three Leo-voice options; the chosen one becomes the notebook's first running theory.
- **Entry 3** (after the handover): the FACT over-tag (§2 beat 8). The system teaches tagging with an entry the player doesn't yet know is wrong.
- **Entry 4** (Day 3 night): first QUESTION: the player picks one of three authored curiosities (the terminal bins; the Works' new lock; why Miss Odessa works two jobs) — *or* writes their own.
- **Entry 5** (Day 7): the first RECALL wobble: one sensory line about a motel hum as he passes the laundromat (F1's long fuse; it fires in C1). Rendered faint, unexplained, three lines max.
- **Map ink:** Terminal → bodega → Corner Slice → the plaza → the cart corner → the fire-escape view. Rosa's handoff note on the Map: *"the usual is a tiny love letter"* (shipped canon line, kept).

## 4. The three hooks (planted, small, no conspiracy)

1. **The lease notice** on Eddie's wall — visible, unremarked. A player who interacts with it gets one line from Eddie: "Paper's just paper till it isn't." (This is C1's fuse, lit in plain sight.)
2. **The surveyor at dawn** — a man with a camera and a sun-angle chart at the Works fence. If asked: "Light studies. Sunlight's a legal requirement. Square feet before feelings." The diary's GUESS ("he's watching someone") is available; it will be struck through in C2 — *it was always sunlight and square feet.* The city's first correction of the player's imagination.
3. **Miss Odessa's second job** — she leaves the gold window's building at 9 p.m. three nights a week. Nobody comments. The game never explains. (It's a home-aide shift; it surfaces once, in C3, when Eli mentions Grandma sleeps on the bus. That's all. Some things are just true.)

## 5. Interactions & systems in the session

Walking/interacting (shipped), the diary write (new), the tag system (new, taught by use), the Map's first pins (shipped + new ink), the postcard pipeline (shipped), the grounding interaction (Day 2, taught), the name/look picker (shipped — runs before beat 0; the pick-card blurbs unchanged; the chosen name is the name the block learns).

## 6. What must NOT happen in the opening (binding)

No crime, no conspiracy, no mention of Corvus by name (the render poster exists on a bus shelter; it is set dressing, unreadable in passing), no flashback, no on-screen medical event, no NPC asks Leo for a favor he can't refuse, no UI lectures, no amnesia exposition beyond the notebook's *facts only* and one sensory wobble. The city teaches; the man performs; the notebook records; that's the whole opening.

## 7. Transitions

- **To the rest of C0 (Days 5–7):** the loop stabilizes — open, rush, roam, wind-down, postcard.
- **To C1 (season cut, Sept 16):** the first interstitial: diary montage (the week's entries in his hand), one postcard (the fire-escape dusk), and the city's first morning-after note: *"The new one remembered the usual."* Then Monday, 6 a.m., Eddie at the door: "Real shift. Leave the tricks in Ohio."
