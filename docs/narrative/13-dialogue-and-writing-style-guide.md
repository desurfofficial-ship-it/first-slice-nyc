## 1. Core principles

1. **The cover-the-name test.** Any line, with the speaker's name removed, must still identify its speaker. If it doesn't, rewrite until it does.
2. **Subtext is the dialogue.** People in this game want things they aren't saying. A scene states its want at most once; everything else is behavior around it.
3. **The city is a character with no lines.** Setting, weather, schedules, and sound carry theme. If a narrator could say it, a fire escape should show it instead.
4. **Conflict through positions, not volume.** Nobody shouts their worldview. The loudest scene in the bible (the funeral porch) is almost silent.
5. **Exposition is a tax.** Pay it in character, in hunger: people explain things they want you to *do* something about.
6. **Feelings-First still governs.** Names arrive after feelings; terms of art (zoning, AMI, deferred prosecution) are learned by touching them, never by lecture.

## 3. Leo's voice arc (chapter by chapter)

- **C0–C1:** the performance is fun and mostly harmless. Jokes outnumber disclosures 10:1. New York errors are comic and frequent (fluency ladder: Tourist → New Arrival).
- **C2:** the jokes learn to carry weight ("net good" enters his vocabulary — the diary's most dangerous phrase). Fluency: Regular.
- **C3:** the voice starts sounding like a man running for something. Third-person self-narration peaks. Fluency: Regular-plus.
- **C4:** the performance voice and the private voice diverge audibly in the same scenes. The jokes get crueler at the exact moments he's kindest. Fluency: Neighbor ("look at you" moment available from Rosa — the designed payoff, kept).
- **C5:** the performance voice dies mid-chapter. Sentences shorten. The last joke of C5 is funny and awful ("I always wanted a basement office.").
- **C6:** humor returns without the audience. Local by fluency. The voice the bible was aiming at: a man who sounds like himself to himself.

**Diary prose trend (measurable, doc 03 §6):** entries shorten ~40% from C3 to C5; joke rate halves; first-person "I" density drops as agency drains, then recovers in C6. Writers should be able to date an entry blind by C4.

## 4. Humor rules

1. Character-specific: every major has a comedy mode (Eddie: deadpan economy; Deshaun: gallows; June: specificity; Priya: announcer-voice; Kev: internet; Rosa: verdicts). No quip-fountains.
2. Punching rules: the block's humor punches up, sideways, and at itself — never down at the displacement, the faith, the condition, or the wounded.
3. Two cruel jokes per chapter, maximum, always camouflage (doc 01 §9) — tracked in the continuity register.
4. The pizza-rat beat (GDD 5.1) survives as the C1 wonder-beat: the whole city filming a rat with a slice is the one moment every faction in the story agrees on. It is also, silently, the title's thesis played for laughter: everyone's hungry; the rat's just honest.

## 5. Staging the hard scenes (betrayal, the villain question, the fall)

1. Small rooms. Soft light. The television off.
2. No character explains the theme; the staging explains it (the last slice on the counter; the unstruck ledger line; the empty stool).
3. Leo's self-justification must be convincing (doc 08 §9). The player may briefly agree. That agreement is the design working.
4. The victim gets the last human line, not the last point.
5. The villain-question scene: one page of reading, no speech; the line is written, not voiced ("The diary doesn't lie. The writer did."); the scene ends on an ordinary action (he labels a GUESS correctly for the first time in the game).
6. The fall is staged administratively (offices, forms, phone calls) — its cruelty is bureaucratic; its mercy is Mercado's walk and Ada's non-question.

## 6. Profanity and content register

- Mature themes, restrained language. Profanity permitted, rare-precious, character-specific (Eddie: two words, both about ovens; Deshaun: workplace vocabulary; Leo: almost never — when he does, it is the scene's thunder).
- Never: slurs, sexual explicitness, on-screen violence, on-screen self-harm (doc 02 §8 governs the railing), cruelty toward faith or displacement played for laughs.
- Heavy-beat resource note fires once, opt-in, first occurrence (doc 14 R12).

## 7. The amnesia in dialogue

- Leo describes gaps in concrete, unclinical language ("the year I can't find," "the tape's blank," "I have the receipt, not the day").
- Others' questions are natural and brief; nobody interrogates; nobody fixes.
- His lies of omission are written so the *player* catches them first — dramatic irony as respect, never as gotcha.
- Dr. Feld's clinical statements are conservative, real-world-accurate, and never promising (doc 02 §4).

## 8. New York authenticity (texture rules)

**Banned:** "I'm walkin' here!" (and other tourist liturgy), pigeon-feeding as character, "fuhgeddaboudit" as seasoning, a Manhattan that is one skyline, cops with one personality, cabbies with one monologue, the pizza as personality transplant.

**Required texture (used as grammar, never as sim):** OMNY taps and the 7:40 platform pileup; alternate-side parking despair; the super's key ring; the M121's schedule as weather; halal carts at 2 a.m.; the bodega's sandwich liturgy ("salt pepper ketchup" as a complete order — shipped canon, kept); washing-machine quarters as currency; stoop rules (you wave, you don't join); "the island of tall evenings" as the *name before the name* for Manhattan (Feelings-First, kept from the legacy laws).

**Dialect discipline:** accents are rhythm and lexicon, never phonetic spelling. Culture is shown through practice (the fajr walk, the dominoes, the pantry logistics, the cookbook) — never through costume dialogue.

## 9. Text messages and screens

Texts are prose artifacts with per-character typing manners (Kev: lowercase, timestamps, screenshots; Marisol: sign-offs from assistants; Craig: paragraphs at 1:14 a.m., unsent drafts shown). The sync screens and newsboxes carry the city's chorus in headline register. The diary's handwriting font is the only "voice" allowed to be beautiful.

## 10. Writing workflow (repo law compliance)

1. All spoken/map/diary prose lives in `src/core/content.ts` tables — the seam law holds: the sim is a seam, tests pin it, renderers only read.
2. Every canon line quoted in docs 02–12 that ships gets a test assertion (AGENTS.md §1c.1: issue → permanent guard). The continuity register (doc 15) lists the protected lines.
3. No prose system may depend on `step ===` logic (the Roam law's ambient clause); narrative triggers fire from the state machine's named transitions.
4. Determinism: progression-adjacent prose (diary montages, chapter-end history pages) must be pure functions of saved state (doc 14 R4).
5. New authored entries pass the cover-the-name test and the diary voice test (doc 03 §9.4) before PR.
