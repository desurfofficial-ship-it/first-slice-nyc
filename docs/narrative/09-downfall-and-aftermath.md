# 09 — Downfall & Aftermath

**The collapse, the continuation, and the question the game refuses to answer for you**

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Canon (v1.0) |
| Depends on | 06 (C5–C6), 08 (cascade architecture) |
| Law | **THE GAME DOES NOT END AT THE FALL.** This document's first and last subject is the life that continues. |

---

## 1. Doctrine

- **The fall fires; the shape is earned.** Rail R5 makes the collapse structural — a man with his leverage profile, his paper trail, and his enemies' independent motives does not walk away clean in this economy. What the player controls is what it *costs*, who it *burns*, and what *survives* (doc 08 §8's modifier table). Design honesty demanded by the brief and owed to the player: the bible states plainly that the fall is load-bearing — its alternative was a story where consequences are optional, and that story would betray every law in doc 01.
- **No melodrama.** No prison, no death, no amnesia deus ex machina, no redemption ceremony. The collapse is *administrative, social, and emotional* — which is how lives actually end.
- **The city is not angry.** It is busy. The aftermath's climate is not punishment; it is *indifference with occasional grace* — the most realistic and most survivable weather there is.

## 2. The cascade (C5, week by week)

**Player-visible off-ramps:** each trigger week contains one authored off-ramp the character doesn't take (a call not returned, a board meeting skipped, a lawyer consulted late). These are *played* as omissions (doc 10 §3), and a player history that made amends early, avoided Gino, or chose N4c honestly *removes or softens specific triggers* — the cascade still fires; it can fire smaller.

## 5. The continuation paths (simultaneously available, never graded)

1. **Rebuild.** The amends ledger; garden work-weeks on Yoli's terms; the hall's community veto board (a C6 institution his restitution funds and his presence is banned from chairing).
2. **Reinvent.** Big Mike's diner; the cookbook chapters; a sanitation-operations certificate (the system that ate him, learned from the inside); teaching the Subway University walks with Priya's cold blessing.
3. **Revenge.** The honest version: testimony at the concessions hearing (optional, real, costs him the last of Marisol's respect and buys the block's CLT option). The dark version: the Wexler stipend file to a reporter — available, consequential, and the game's final villain-exam re-run: it *works*, nothing improves, and Gino's fruit basket stops coming. The world's reaction is mixed, tired, alive.
4. **Reconcile.** Doc 08 §7's dial table governs every door. Nothing opens by cutscene.
5. **Ordinary.** The LIC diner, the basement, a Thursday league, a library card. The game supports a small life with zero irony and full content.
6. **Quiet.** Eddie's floor in Charlotte is available *as a persistent option*, not an ending: he can visit, he can stay a season, the city will hold his place the way cities do — not at all, and completely.

**Forbidden (canon violations if built):** full memory recovery; prison; death; a forgiveness ceremony; a "good/evil" ending grade; marriage as resolution; the block apologizing to him.

## 6. The post-fall world state (C6 open)

- **Juniper Commons** rises: the hall opens in spring Y4 — his stall layout, her lobby, his name in no brochure. The render's 5:45 sun was a rendering choice; the real one sets when it sets.
- **The coalition wins its concessions** (280 affordable units, the CLT option on the garden slot) with the Compact papers — Yoli's victory, doc 07 §2.3; his testimony optional.
- **The row:** Corvus-owned. Corner Slice's space becomes a chain slice shop, same prices, worse pie; the ritual is not for sale at any price and the new operators don't know it existed. The Cecil stands as "managed workforce housing"; his old room rents to a nursing student.
- **People:** Eddie in Charlotte, alive, weekly calls (length = amends-state meter, diegetic); Rosa still load-bearing; Deon still refusing; Deniz sitting the CPA exam; Kev on one of two shelves (doc 07 §2.4); June on one of two shelves; Priya transferring lines — the K keeps her; the handover keeps her human; Craig in Lauderdale with a sticky note in the mail; Doris's ledger in Rosa's cigar box.
- **The block:** the mural repainted over the LIAR tag (Lena paints the Ghost Sign restored above it — "the block's memory, in paint, of what was there before"); the court resurfaced; Kestrel Green's hundredth tree planted by whoever the player has become; the flyer board still rotating its papers; the barrier diary's asphalt long since faded; Mango immune.
- **The city:** indifferent, occasionally kind. The 7:40 still comes. The soup was good, though. October, remember?

## 7. The late tests (C6's set pieces)

1. **Marisol's offer** ("Come back when you want to be good at this again.") — accepting re-runs the villain exam with better production values; declining with grace is the peace option; *both are supported; neither is graded.*
2. **Eddie's floor** — the exit that is also a home; taking it changes the map, not the person.
3. **The self-portrait** (doc 03 §7) — the arc's decision-form; F8 fires inside it; the diary's last main-arc line is the player's, not ours.

## 8. The formal close and the open end

The main arc's window formally closes around **June Y4** — the nine-month mark — with a final arc beat: *The Debts* complete or explicitly open, the self-portrait written or refused (refusal is a canon-valid state the city's diary comments on once, gently). After that, no endscreen exists: the simulation continues; the seasons continue; the city's diary keeps its one-note-per-morning law. The game's answer to "what is he now?" is always the same sentence, re-derivable any day: **whatever the ledger, the map, and the people who still set a stool out say it is.**

— Lead Narrative Director, 2026-11-06
