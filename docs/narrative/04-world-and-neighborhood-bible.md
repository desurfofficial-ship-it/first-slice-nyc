## 1. The setting in one paragraph

Alder Heights is a working upper-Manhattan neighborhood — the ridge between Harlem and Washington Heights as the map would say, except the map doesn't bother, which the residents consider a compliment. One commercial street (Kestrel) stitched between two avenues (Juniper with the K under it, Sycamore with the diner sign), brownstones stepping down the north slope, walk-ups and a bakery row to the south, the old Alder Works hall holding the block's best ghost sign, three wooden water towers keeping the skyline honest. The registry's "October, golden hour, forever" is the arrival chapter's mood; from Chapter 1 the seasons move (doc 12). It is a place where everyone is having an ordinary Tuesday — which is exactly what makes it possible to break.

## 3. History (the ledger under the neighborhood)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1921 | **Alder Works** opens: kitchen cabinets and fixtures. The ghost sign is painted. |
| 1930s | The Works employs 400; the avenues fill with walk-ups; Gable Row builds its stoop culture. |
| 1962–1988 | The Hollis family assembles its eleven buildings on the Kestrel south row (Corner Slice's block, Rosa's, the Cecil, the laundry bay, the Halcyon shell). rents stay "friend prices" for decades. |
| 1974–1990 | Manufacturing bleeds out; the Works runs three shifts, then one; the fires of the late 70s take two Marlow lots (the playground is built on one). |
| **1994** | **The Alder Compact.** A community land trust nearly happens: the Works parcel, air rights, co-op conversions for the rowhouses. Hartwell Corp (a 1990s development outfit) counter-offers; **Ernie Ferreira**, the Works' shop steward and Tito's older brother, delivers a counter-petition (214 signatures, nine of them Gable Row families) that kills the CLT vote. The Compact dies in a community-board meeting that older residents still call "the night the rain came inside." Ernie takes a "consulting position." Hartwell busts in the 2008–2011 wave with the option unexercised; title tangles for a decade. |
| 1999 | The Works closes. 400 jobs, then a chain-link fence, then pigeons. |
| 2000s–2010s | Immigrant renewal: the Dominican bodega economy, the Oktays take Kestrel Hardware (31 years by the story's start), Masjid an-Nur opens, the diner changes hands to Big Mike, artists find cheap square footage in the Works' edge spaces. |
| Y-3 | **Corvus Group** begins assembling: the Works parcel, Marlow-edge lots, option tendrils near the Hollis buildings. The rezoning rumor starts. |
| Y1, Sept | Leo arrives. The block does not notice for nine days. |

The Alder Compact is the story's buried rhyme (docs 06 C3, 08): the neighborhood was betrayed once, by one of its own, for reasons that looked reasonable at the dinner table. The protagonist's arc re-runs the test with the roles reversed.

## 4. Social structure

- **Population (in-world figure, flagged fictional):** ~9,800. Roughly a third Dominican and Puerto Rican families (some three generations deep), a quarter Black households with Southern and Caribbean roots, a fifth Mexican and Central American families, and a long tail: West African, Yemeni, South Asian, Albanian, Irish and Italian remnants, and the first wave of the new arrivals — young, lease-signed, tote-bagged, coming for the rents that haven't moved yet.
- **Classes in friction:** legacy homeowners (the rowhouses), long renters on friend prices (the Hollis buildings), market-rate newcomers (Sycamore Studios' tenants), and the un-housed margin the story never makes decorative (the terminal's overnight benches).
- **Institutions:** the Ascension Church (Father Mike — the parish's boiler fund will matter, doc 09), Masjid an-Nur (Imam Dawan; Idris's five-a.m. crowd), P.S. 218 (Camille Odom's classroom; Principal Constance Hill), the Sycamore branch library (El Vann's old reading room), the community board (deliberately unnumbered in canon — it is always "the board"), the dominoes table at Corner Park (Tito's senate — see doc 07 §4), the half-court (Deshaun, Darnell, and the summer league), JMA — **Juniper Mutual Aid** (Yoli's coalition: the garden, the freezer, the flyer network, the part-time organizer stipend that never covers the hours).
- **How news travels (gossip physics, implementable):** the dominoes table is the senate; the barbershop and the laundry are the wire services; the bodega counter is the court of appeals; the K platform is where all editions meet at 7:40 a.m. Anything the player does in public is editorial by evening and front-page by Sunday. Private acts stay private exactly as long as nobody is hurt enough to talk.

## 6. Local politics and the conflict: Juniper Commons

**The parties — every one has independent interests, and none is a cartoon:**

- **Corvus Group** (developer). Principal **Marisol Quintero** (45, Sunset Park-born, genuinely believes she is saving the block from decay and is ruthless about the block that fits her render). Fixer **Dane Wexler** (38, ex-Department of Buildings examiner; knows which violations are real). The project: **Juniper Commons** — ~900 units on the Alder Works parcel and adjacent lots, 20% "affordable" (AMI math the block will spend two chapters learning to read), a food hall ("Provisions Hall"), a plaza with the render's the sun always at 5:45 PM.
- **The Hollis family** (landlords). Matriarch **Doris**, 78 — eleven buildings, friend prices, dialysis two days a week, taxes and boilers closing in. Son **Craig**, 52 — failed in Florida, back, embarrassed, the easy mark: SaniHaul buys his debt in C4 and he spends C5 selling the family out one door at a time.
- **Juniper Mutual Aid** (the coalition). **Yolanda "Yoli" Ferreira** and the Gable Garden, the freezer program, theCompact's-paper trail. Their assets are people, documents, and patience; their weakness is that patience is not a lease term.
- **SaniHaul of New York** (carting, "waste logistics"). The quiet old-money of demolition: they need the Works' hauling contracts and the church's parking-lot lease. Front man **Gino Trabucco** — reasonable, well-read, feeds you at lunch and files your violations away like coupons. Behind him, the Ferrante family: phones only, a voice named **Anton**. They are never shown committing a crime on-screen; they are shown making problems *arrangeable*.
- **City machinery.** Council Member **Alma Reyes** (61, three terms, endorses whoever the room claps for), her chief of staff Kayla Fournier, and a community board that genuinely tries and genuinely tires.

**The process the block learns to read (in-world civics arc, C2–C3):** rezoning application → environmental assessment → community board review → the council vote → permits → the crane. Yoli teaches it at the garden in plain language; Priya teaches the subway version at the station; the game never lectures (No-Quiz Law) — the process is drama: deadlines, quorums, sign-in sheets.

**Why no one is the villain:** Corvus's money is real and so are the boilers it would replace; Doris's friend prices are love and also a maintenance deficit; Yoli's coalition is right about displacement and wrong about leverage twice; SaniHaul's trucks are legal at 6 a.m.; the council member's earmarks fix the playground. The conflict is a machine everyone is feeding for their own honest reasons — doc 01's Slice Law with a crane on top.

## 7. The world's independent calendar (the City-Moves Law made concrete)

Events that proceed regardless of the player, keyed to chapters (full detail in doc 06 §12, ages in doc 12):

- **C1:** the Halloween nor'easter (Oct 29–Nov 1) takes the block's power for four days; the mural's second figure goes up; Eddie's lease renewal notice arrives (first one).
- **C2:** the Oktays lose Kestrel Hardware (May); Rosa's 25th anniversary party (June); Craig's rent re-rating wave; Kev's community-college appeal denied (his money pressure, independent of Leo).
- **C3:** Eddie's heart attack (Jul 6); Kestrel Green's pergola finished; the garden loses its water access for eleven days (a city paperwork snag nobody caused); Alma Reyes's earmark rescues the playground swings.
- **C4:** the jobs-fair backlash; Camille transfers to a Bronx school (her own decision); Deon's shop gets its first market-rate offer and refuses it; the mural gets tagged and restored.
- **C5:** Doris dies (Sept 14); the Works' hoarding goes up; the "LIAR" tag; the first scaffold riser on the Commons site.
- **C6:** the food hall opens (without Leo); the coalition's compact papers force Corvus's concessions (Yoli's win — theirs, not his); Kestrel Green gets its hundredth tree; the block keeps a light on.

## 8. What Alder Heights is not

Not a borough, not a theme park of famous places, not a map of real addresses. Real New York exists as *context and conversation* — the K line, OMNY, the 7:40, halal carts, the courts in Harlem, the ferries, Inwood's last cheap rooms — while the playable neighborhood is a fictional composite with fictional names, fictional numbers, and true physics. All institutional names (P.S. 218, the Ascension Church, Masjid an-Nur, the board) are fictional; the doc 15 register carries the standing flag so no real institution is ever defamed or misrepresented by story events.

— Lead Narrative Director, 2026-11-06
