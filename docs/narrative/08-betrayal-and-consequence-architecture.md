## 1. Doctrine

1. **Betrayal is structural, not scheduled.** The story requires that Leo betray before he is betrayed (R2, R5). What the rails require is the *event class*; the player chooses its *form*, its victims, and its depth. Nothing is punished on a clock: consequences fire when their causes mature (Ledger Law).
2. **Every sacrifice is sincerely justified in the moment and priced honestly afterward.** The game never winks at the player while Leo self-justifies. The diary records the justification as if it were true; the *world* holds the invoice.
3. **Not every betrayal destroys.** Some relationships bend. Some relationships end. One relationship (Deshaun) invoices rather than breaks. The repair dials (§7) are per-victim, never global.
4. **The victim is never a prop.** Every victim of Leo's choices has, in doc 07, a full life in which the betrayal is one entry in their own ledger. The Oktays are not "the cost"; they are people whose year this event ruined.
5. **No cartoon evil.** Marisol is not a villain; SaniHaul commits no on-screen crime; Craig is pathetic before he is destructive; and Leo is the story's most effective antagonist — an antagonist with a diary.

## 2. The appetite ladder (how a person becomes this)

The betrayals do not arrive from nowhere; each is one step taller than the last, and each step is *narrated to Leo by Leo* as reasonable:

1. **Y1:** the over-tagged FACT entries (doc 03 §2) — he lies first to his own notebook, about small things (the cart; the ease of a shift). The player watches the tag system get gamed before the player watches anything get stolen.
2. **C1:** fixing by access (the June DOB wall) — solving problems with relationships instead of labor. Legal, generous, and the exact muscle the Compact break trains.
3. **C2:** **Betrayal I.** For security. Sincerely mixed motives.
4. **C3:** the Gino lunches — problems made *arrangeable*. He tells himself it's networking. The diary's refusal to record the violation (doc 06 C3, event 6) is the tell the player is invited to catch.
5. **C4:** **Betrayal II.** For the future. Deliberate, at scale, wearing mentorship.
6. **C5:** the cascade. Not a betrayal by Leo — a betrayal *of* Leo — assembled from the parts he supplied.

## 3. Betrayal I — the Compact break (full spec)

**Situation.** Eleven businesses sign Yoli's Compact: unified renewal, mutual support, no-sell. Its power is unanimity. Craig's assemblage needs it broken; Corvus's timeline prefers it broken; Leo's shop needs *saving*, and Leo alone is offered the ribbon.

**The node (N2, doc 06 C2).** Three authored forms, all of which satisfy rail R2:

- **Form A (canon default), "The quiet pull-out":** Leo withdraws Corner Slice alone. The Compact loses its anchor merchant's visible loyalty; two waverers follow within weeks; Craig flips the hardware unit; the Oktays are displaced in May. Leo's five-year friend-rate arrives with a bow.
- **Form B, "The courier":** Leo pulls out *and* carries two more signatures out with him (a cleaner and a deli owner whose leases he knows through the shop). Deeper betrayal, better terms, faster Oktay collapse. The receipts (doc 05 §2.14) contain his text: *"meeting went fine. tell your mom I tried."*
- **Form C, "The information trade":** Leo refuses to withdraw but trades the Compact's internal strategy schedule to Priya's office "to help the board prepare" — it reaches Craig, the Compact is met individually and dismantled in a week. Leo keeps clean hands and a clean lease and *knows*. The Oktays still pay. This form is for players who want Leo to keep his self-image intact the longest; the fall prices this form hardest (doc 09 §2: denial is a debt too).

**His reasoning, in his own register (for writers):** "The Compact was eleven businesses pretending eleven businesses have power. Craig had already toured three of our buildings. Yoli's plan was a circle holding hands on a hill. Eddie's shop is *actual* — people, pies, forty years. I protected the real thing. I'm sorry the real thing isn't everybody. Somebody was always going to eat last."

**The victim's perspectives (write these as true):**
- **Mehmet Oktay:** thirty-one years of inventory arithmetic gone in one afternoon of boxes. He does not blame Leo louder than he blames Craig; he simply *includes* him in the sentence now, forever, in the same breath: "the Hollis boy and the new boy."
- **Deniz:** converts grief to documentation the same week. Her receipts start here — not as revenge, as *habit* (doc 05 §2.14).
- **Yoli:** loses the strategy, audits herself, survives — and files Leo under *conditions that break compacts*, a category that later includes her uncle. She does not know she's filed him next to Ernie until C4, when Tito's story shows her the folder was never empty.
- **Rosa:** says nothing. Feeds him anyway. The toast (doc 06 C2) is her verdict.
- **Eddie:** knows — old men know — and asks the question he already knows the answer to. His acceptance of the lease is the chapter's most painful kindness: relief without ignorance.

**Consequences curve (delayed by design):** nothing bad happens for months. The Compact is a slow ruin: the Oktays (May), then the laundromat's hours cut (C3), then the general knowledge (C4–C5) — the neighborhood never punishes Leo socially for C2; it *prices* him. The punishing arrives when the paper does.

**What it changes in him:** he learns that betrayal works. This is the story's most expensive lesson and the ladder's next step is built with it.

**Repair paths (§7 dials):** Yoli — partial, professional, C6-garden terms. Deniz — the deposition only. Mehmet & Ayşe — never, and the game says so (doc 09 §7). Eddie — full, via the last night. Rosa — never broken, permanently recalibrated.

## 4. The Gino lunches (organized-adjacent texture, restrained)

Two lunches total (C3, C4). The first makes a stale violation disappear; the second "looks into" a competitor's stall application for the hall. On-screen: food, warmth, naval history, and hospitality. Off-screen: arrangement. The bible's rules for this thread: no on-screen crimes; no on-screen threats; no guns; no money changing hands in scene; the horror is entirely in *availability* — of Gino, of lunch, of a man who makes problems arrangeable and never asks for anything until the cascade, when what he asks for is a community letter Leo once wrote supporting a carting-fee schedule change (C4, one signature, buried in a stack), which becomes Trigger 4's public face. Leo's line at the investigation: "It was a letter about *garbage*." The investigator's reply is not written for this bible; doc 13 gives it to the actors: "Most things are, until they're not."

**Eddie's own Gino-era** (doc 07 §2.1): the DOB ghost Leo made disappear was originally *Eddie's* problem, absorbed in Y-30 the same way. The ritual slice has old paper under it too. This is the inheritance theme's quietest statement, and it must never be said out loud in scene — El Vann comes closest: "Documents don't fail, dear. They wait."

## 5. Betrayal II — Kev and the jobs number (full spec)

**The mechanics.** The pledge video needs a local face. Marisol's deck says 340 condition-dependent jobs. Leo, on camera-adjacent instinct, says "five hundred" — because round numbers fund, because the *direction* is true, because Kev's sincere face is the best evidence a lie can wear. Leo's private arithmetic: "The number's a forecast. Forecasts get revised. By the time anyone audits it, the hall's open and the revision is my problem, not the kid's."

**The victim's perspective.** Kev believed the number because he believed Leo. The audit lands in April; the clip becomes a meme; Grace watches it at a client's house on mute. Kev's words, once, at the shop door: "You didn't even need me to lie. You needed me to be *believable*." The screenshots folder gets a new subfolder that night, named `in case`.

**Why it's the worse betrayal.** Betrayal I was survival dressed as principle. Betrayal II is appetite dressed as mentorship. The story's ladder is legible in the victims: strangers (Oktays) → the kid who trusted him (Kev) → the woman who loved him (June, the diary discovery, same chapter). Each betrayal spends a more intimate currency.

**The consequences.** Kev → JMA's hard line (C4); the receipts → the post (C5, Trigger 2); the block's memory of the Face's face: a kid's, lying. Kev's radicalization is *rational*, funded by Leo's sincerity problem, and never narratively corrected — doc 07 §2.4 keeps his resentment earned.

## 6. The mirror: 1994, and the villain-question scene

**The Ernie mirror (C4, Tito's story).** The mechanism of the reframe is not "Leo = Ernie" as a label; it is the *sentence*: "The block was going to lose anyway. I saved what I could." — Ernie's, delivered to Tito thirty years ago, and Leo's own K2 entry, read aloud in Dr. Feld's office two weeks later, half-recognizing whose cadence it has. The bible forbids any character drawing the equation explicitly. The player assembles it. That is the difference between drama and lecture.

**The villain-question scene (C4, mid-June; R4 fires here; full staging rules in doc 13 §6):**
- **Trigger assembly (all required, all organic):** K2 and K4 read side by side; Tito's story fresh; Deniz at the bar; June's absence; the therapy appointment circled twice on the Map and not attended.
- **The scene:** 2 a.m., the shop dark, the K-Globes amber through the gate. Leo opens the notebook to K2 (C2: "Net good.") and finds his own handwriting *unfamiliar* — he checks the date, checks the ink, checks the corner where he signs. It is his. The scene is one page of him reading his own record like evidence. No speech. The only line, written, not spoken: *"The diary doesn't lie. The writer did."*
- **Aftermath rules:** no redemption toggle; no alignment flip; no confession cutscene. He doubles down in public (realistic) and starts therapy in private (also realistic). The question — *has he become the villain?* — is never answered by the game. It is answered by the player's own next twenty hours of play, which is the design's whole thesis.

## 7. The repair dials

Three separate dials per victim, never conflated: **Repair** (the practical debt: money, letters, depositions, hours), **Forgiveness** (the emotional settlement: freely given or never), **Restoration** (the relationship as it was: mostly impossible, and the game says so). Table:

| Victim | Repair possible | Forgiveness possible | Restoration possible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oktays (Mehmet & Ayşe) | Money, refused; the deposition accepted | **No.** Canon, final | No |
| Deniz | The deposition | Cold professional respect, C6 | No |
| Kev | Reference letter, in person | A civil working relationship, C6, on the amends path | No |
| June | The three conditions + the reckoning | Conditional, slow, C6 shift-employment | A new thing, never the old thing |
| Yoli | Garden work-weeks | "I don't forgive people. I assess them." | Professional alliance only |
| Deshaun | The invoice (6 months, then the booth) | Yes, always could | Yes, changed |
| Eddie | "Come see me when you're done being sorry." | Yes | The floor in Charlotte, not the shop |
| Rosa | Never broken | Not hers to give or withhold — she *assesses* | Recalibrated permanently |
| Ada / the $8,000 | Restitution, first dollar | Never doubted him. That's the wound | Yes — the only restoration in the game that costs nothing but truth |
| Verna / Idris / Miss Odessa / the Club's nineteen | The amends ledger (doc 09 §4) | Per-person, mostly quiet, never celebrated | Small, real, partial |

## 8. The betrayal-in-return: cascade triggers and their weights

Full staging in doc 09 §2; the architecture here. Five triggers, each independently caused, each with a weight modified by the player's history — the fall's *event* is structural (R5), its *shape* is earned:

| # | Trigger | Owner | Caused by | Player-history modifiers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Construction delay (rate environment + the coalition's suit) | The machine, not a person | The conflict itself | Yoli's suit is stronger if N4c gave her the El Vann location |
| 2 | The receipts | Kev → Deniz | Betrayal II + the screenshots habit | Amends made before C5 (any) soften the post's tone; none harden it |
| 3 | The handover | Priya → the council office | Reyes's need; Priya's pricing | Impossible if Leo never traded intel with her; possible-but-colder if he did (canon default) |
| 4 | The call | SaniHaul/Ferrantes | The C4 letter signature | Removed entirely on no-Gino paths; replaced by a pure margin failure |
| 5 | The run | Nineteen investors | Triggers 2–4 made public | Investor-list choice (N3b) sets who is hurt worst |

**Design rule:** no trigger is a *betrayal* in the melodrama sense — every betrayer acts from independent, documented motivation (docs 05/07): Kev's `in case` folder, Priya's neutrality finally priced, Gino's coupon-file, the investors' arithmetic. The brief's requirement — the betrayer's independent motivations — is satisfied by construction: everyone was always going to do this; Leo was the variable, not the cause.

## 9. Writing rules for the hard scenes

1. Subtext over speech: no scene states its moral; the staging does (doc 13 §6).
2. The victim gets the last *human* line, not the last *point*.
3. Leo's justifications are written to be **convincing**. If a player can't briefly agree with him, the writing failed.
4. The cost curve is delayed and social, never instant and karmic (Ledger Law).
5. One mercy per chapter, from the unlikeliest holder (C2: Eddie's acceptance; C3: Doris's door warning; C4: Deshaun's non-keynote; C5: Mercado's walk to the station; C6: Deniz's deposition terms).
