# FIRST SLICE — Paper Prototype Kit
**In-person + Remote versions**

Use this kit to test the two core feelings of the game before writing more code or building greybox.

**Time required:** 15–20 minutes per person/group  
**People needed:** 3–5 (mix of “never been to NYC” and cozy-game players if possible)

---

## 1. Facilitator One-Pager (say this)

### Opening (30 seconds)
> “We’re testing how it *feels* to arrive in New York as a complete newcomer. There are no wrong answers. You can’t fail anything. I’m just going to walk you through a short morning in the city.”

### During the test
- Never say the words “tutorial,” “minigame,” or “system.”
- If someone looks stuck, smile and say: “The city is patient.”
- Only teach when they ask or clearly hesitate.
- Let silence exist. Don’t fill every gap.

### Closing questions (ask exactly these)
1. Did any moment feel like a test or a quiz?
2. Did you feel welcomed or corrected?
3. Which moment do you remember most clearly?
4. Would you open Map again tomorrow?
5. In one sentence: how did the city treat you?

Write down their exact words. Those phrases are gold.

## 2. In-Person Kit

### What to print / prepare
- 1 folded piece of paper per person (Map)
- Customer cards (cut these out or write on index cards)
- Pen

### Map Setup (do this before people arrive)
Fold a piece of paper once.  
On the inside left page write only:

> You are here.  
> That’s enough.

Leave the right side almost blank (light pencil margins are ok).

### Customer Cards (copy these)

**Card 1 – Regular Coffee**  
“Regular coffee.”  
*(If they hesitate)* “Milk and two sugars. That’s what regular means here.”

**Card 2 – Bacon Egg & Cheese**  
“Bacon egg and cheese. Salt, pepper, ketchup.”  
*(After)* “That whole sentence is a complete order. You’re speaking New York now.”

**Card 3 – Mr. Idris**  
“The usual.”  
*(Quietly)* “Two sugars. Every day for nine years. You’ll see him tomorrow.”

**Card 4 – The Kid**  
“Whatever’s cold… I don’t really know.”  
Player may: just make something / ask / say “Chef’s welcome.”

**Card 5 – Optional stretch**  
“Chopped cheese on a hero, letta, tomahta, hots.”  
Explain only one piece if they’re lost. Never all at once.

### Player Choices (always available)
- Say nothing and just make it
- Repeat the order back
- Ask one question
- Say “Chef’s welcome”

### Flow (15 minutes)
1. Hand them Map folded. Say nothing about it.
2. Run 3–5 customer cards.
3. At one teach moment, pause and say: “Map wants to write this down. Want to see?”
4. If they open it, write the note slowly in ordinary handwriting.
5. End on the fire-escape / gold window narration:
   > “Later that evening you climb the fire escape with a coffee. One window across the way flickers, then stays gold. Rosa says quietly: ‘That one’s the kid who ordered whatever’s cold. You were their first warm thing today.’”
6. Ask the five closing questions.

### Recommended Form Structure

**Section 1 – Arrival**  
You step off a bus. A woman named Rosa notices your map is upside down and laughs kindly. She offers you breakfast in exchange for helping her for a few minutes.

How does this opening make you feel? (short text)

**Section 2 – Three Orders**  
(Present the first three customer cards one after another. After each, ask:  
“What do you do?” with options:  
- Just make it  
- Repeat the order back  
- Ask a question  
- Say “Chef’s welcome”)  

After the third order (Mr. Idris), show the line:  
“Two sugars. Every day for nine years. You’ll see him tomorrow.”

**Section 3 – Map**  
At one point Map (a folded paper) wants to write something down.  
Do you open it? (Yes / No / Maybe later)

**Section 4 – Ending**  
You end the day on a fire escape. One window turns gold. Someone tells you it belongs to the uncertain kid you served earlier. You were their first warm thing.

**Final Questions**  
1. Did any moment feel like a test or a quiz?  
2. Did you feel welcomed or corrected?  
3. Which moment do you remember most clearly?  
4. Would you open Map again tomorrow?  
5. In one sentence: how did the city treat you?

After 3–5 people, look for patterns. Especially watch for:
- Whether “the usual” lands
- Whether Map feels like a friend or a menu
- Whether anyone uses the word “kind,” “patient,” or “welcoming”

---

## 5. What Good Results Look Like

Green flags:
- People smile at the “usual” moment
- They describe Map as a notebook or companion
- They use words like kind, patient, warm, gentle
- They remember the gold window or the kid

Red flags:
- Anyone says “it felt like a tutorial”
- People feel stupid for not knowing orders
- Map is ignored or called a menu/quest log
- No emotional reaction to the final window
