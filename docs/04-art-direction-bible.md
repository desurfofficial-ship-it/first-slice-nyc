# FIRST SLICE: A New York Story
## Document 04 — Art Direction Bible

*The visual thesis: **a postcard drawn by someone who loves this city.** Not a simulation of New York — a love letter to the idea of it, rendered in clean lowpoly 3D that any player on Earth can read in one second.*

---

## 1. Style Statement

**Clean, colorful, highly readable lowpoly 3D.**

- **Chunky, friendly proportions** — slightly oversized awnings, slightly squat taxis, slightly round pigeons. Everything 5–10% softer than reality.
- **Strong silhouettes** — every landmark, NPC, and prop must be identifiable as a *solid black shape*. The Empire State Building, the water tower, the steam shovel of a coffee cart: all must read at 64 pixels.
- **Flat-shaded surfaces** with soft gradient skies — no realistic textures, no grit. Surfaces carry *color* and *light*, not noise.
- **The city is a diorama you can hold** — the whole world feels like it sits on a bodega shelf.

**Anti-references (what this is NOT):** not gritty-realistic, not hyper-detailed, not desaturated "urban drama," not sepia nostalgia. **Tone references:** warm animated films, diorama miniature games, travel-poster illustration.

---

## 2. The Postcard Warm Palette

| Color | Hex | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Taxi Yellow** | `#F7B32B` | Primary accent | Energy, CTAs, your cart's awning starts here |
| **Brick Red** | `#B23A48` | Secondary | Building faces, warmth, the "heart" color |
| **Fire-Escape Green** | `#2A9D8F` | Tertiary | Awnings, shutters, details — the calm note |
| **Golden-Hour Amber** | `#FFB85C` | The light | The default time of day is 5:45 PM, forever |
| **Steam Grey** | `#E8E4DC` | Atmosphere | Steam, snow, receipt paper, morning haze |
| **Dusk Plum** | `#6D4C7D` | Night | Shadows with warmth — never pure black |
| **Bodega Neon** | `#FF6B6B` | Magic punctuation | Open signs, wonder beats — used sparingly |
| **Pizza-Box White** | `#FFF8F0` | Paper & UI | Postcards, menus, backgrounds |

**Sky law:** every sky is a gradient — peach→periwinkle by day, amber→plum by dusk. The city is never under a flat sky. **The light is a character.**

**Color proportion rule (the 70/20/10 law):** 70% warm neutrals (brick, cream, amber light), 20% identity colors (yellow, green, red), 10% magic (neon, plum, gold shafts). Wonder beats temporarily invert this — that's why they feel like events.

---

## 3. Camera Feel

- **Cozy 3/4 isometric** for gameplay: close enough to read a face, wide enough to read a street. Diorama tilt ~35°.
- **Vertical-first composition:** every scene composes in 9:16 as well as landscape. This is the TikTok law and it overrides everything else.
- **Postcard Mode:** when a wonder beat triggers, the camera auto-frames — gentle letterbox, soft push-in, title card, one-tap save/share. *The game composes your memories for you.*
- **Two-finger rotate & pinch:** the player can hold the city and turn it like an object. (Diorama psychology = ownership psychology.)
- **The camera never shakes.** The city is confident. Even the subway ride home is a smooth, lulling glide.

---

## 4. Light

- **Golden-hour bias:** the default day state is late afternoon, forever 5:45 PM — long soft shadows, honeyed faces, brick at its warmest.
- **Streetlight pools:** at night, scenes live inside circles of warm light. What's lit is what matters.
- **Neon as punctuation only:** bodega signs, theater marquees, the "OPEN" flip — magic moments get neon; daily life gets tungsten.
- **Manhattanhenge shafts:** down-the-grid light, twice a year, the palette's superpower moment.
- **Rain doubles the city:** wet cobblestones reflect every light source. Rain is beauty, never misery.
- **Steam is alive:** manholes, coffee cups, griddle queues, winter breath — steam animates on a global rhythm so no frame is ever dead. **The Rule of Steam: something in every frame must be moving.**

---

## 5. Visual Language & Props

**The NYC object canon** (each drawn once, loved forever):
water towers · fire escapes · steam · yellow cabs · the bodega awning · neon OPEN · produce crates out front · the bagel's impossible size · pizza by the sheet · the halal cart's umbrellas · brownstone stoops · rooftop tanks at dusk · the ferry deck rail · subway tile mosaics · the MTA roundel · pigeons with attitude · strings of lights on a fire escape

**Scale law:** one "hero prop" per screen gets 20% extra size — the eye always knows where the story is.

**People:** round, warm, varied, expressive. Faces read at distance via shape + palette, not detail. Every Regular has one iconic silhouette element (Rosa's apron knot, Priya's cap angle, Idris's paper bag).

---

## 6. UI Language — "It came off the bodega shelf"

- **Receipt-paper panels** with torn edges and dot-matrix type for lists and orders
- **Deli-ticker monospace** for prices and order tickets
- **Postcards with real paper grain**, deckled edges, a stamp corner, and a handwritten-caption font
- **Sticker & washi-tape motifs** for badges and highlights — achievements are *stuck on*, not unlocked
- **Hand-drawn marker arrows** guide the eye during tutorials (drawn "live," like a friend annotating your map)
- **"THE USUAL?" is a real button** — the most important button in the game; it must feel like a handshake
- Haptics are warm: apron-tying buzz, key-jingle pulse, postcard-stamp *thunk*

---

## 7. The 1-Second Law

> **Every frame must be readable, beautiful, and identifiable as "New York" within one second of a paused scroll.**

Discipline checklist for every scene shipping:
1. Silhouette test at 64px?
2. Palette within the 70/20/10 law?
3. One hero prop, clearly the story?
4. Something moving (Rule of Steam)?
5. Composes in 9:16?
6. Would a person who's never been to NYC say "oh, New York!" in one second?

If any answer is no, the scene ships again.

---

## 8. Audio Direction (the invisible half of art)

- **Subway-rumble bass** under everything — the city's heartbeat
- **Neighborhood scoring:** jazz uptown, dembow in the Heights, indie across the bridge, strings in Chinatown — crossing a border is an audible event
- **Softened sirens** — present, ambient, never alarming
- **Multilingual chatter** — the city sounds like the world
- **The Key Jingle** — dawn's Pavlovian comfort cue
- **Hiss-pour-thunk** — the coffee cart's three-note percussion, subtly the game's most repeated sound and most missed when absent

---

## 9. Screenshot Direction (for the marketing team)

The game must *contain* its own marketing. Per-season "postcard key art" is generated from actual gameplay Postcard Mode frames — no renders that the game can't reproduce. The store page, the ads, and the player's own album should be **indistinguishable in beauty**. If a player's screenshot can be mistaken for official key art, the art direction has succeeded.
