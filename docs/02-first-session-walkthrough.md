# FIRST SLICE: A New York Story
## Document 02 — First Session Walkthrough (Minutes 0–30)

*A minute-by-minute script of the opening experience, with dialogue samples, wonder-beat placement, and design rationale. The first session's job: install the fantasy, the loop, the ritual, and the return trigger — while making the player feel the city being kind to them.*

> **v2.0 note:** This script now includes **Map** (the co-newbie companion, GDD §4.0) in the arrival, Subway University, and the first **gold window** of the Skyline of Lit Windows (GDD §6.4).

---

## Design Goals for Session One

1. **No failure states.** Nothing can be botched. Timing is forgiving; every mini-game has a "the city's patient" grace mode.
2. **Characters teach, never menus.** Every instruction comes out of a person's mouth with warmth or humor.
3. **Two guaranteed wonder beats** (Times Square at ~minute 12, skyline dusk at ~minute 28).
4. **One complete emotional arc:** arrival → kindness → capability → ownership → belonging.
5. **The share ritual is experienced, never explained** — the first postcard simply *happens to* the player.
6. **End on an open loop:** "the regulars are waiting."

---

## 0:00–2:00 — THE ARRIVAL

**The screen opens on a bus window.** Countryside slides by. A passenger in a netted trucker hat sleeps against the glass. The driver's radio murmurs a ball game. Then — through the window, far off — the skyline. Just for two seconds. The music starts, quietly.

> **DRIVER:** First time?

*(The player picks from a one-tap response: "How could you tell?" / "Is it that obvious?" / "...Yeah.")*

> **DRIVER:** Everybody's got a look. Like they're memorizin' it already. *(beat)* Don't worry. It'll still be there when you get off.

The bus pulls into the terminal. Title card over the doors opening:

<div align="center"><b>FIRST SLICE</b><br><i>A New York Story</i></div>

You step out with **$87**, a duffel bag, and a face full of city. Six starter characters to pick from (30 seconds, all delightful — skateboard-toting, paint-splattered, grandmotherly, etc.). Movement tutorial is diegetic: walk toward the light.

In your other hand: **Map**. It unfolds itself — impatient paper. Almost nothing is marked. One star in the middle, in patient handwriting:

> *"You are here. That's enough."*

> **Design note:** The skyline tease is *wonder beat #0* — a promise. Map's first note sets the game's whole teaching contract in five words. The driver's line sets the voice: warm, wry, unhurried. The response choice establishes immediately that dialogue is light, short, and never penalizes.

---

## 2:00–5:00 — ROSA'S HAND

Outside the terminal, the city is loud (softened sirens, chatter, the rumble). You stare at a map. It's upside-down. It has been upside-down for an indeterminate time.

> **ROSA:** *(amused, holding two mangoes)* You've had that thing backwards for a whole minute. I was giving you a chance to figure it out.

> **YOU:** Is it... upside-down?

> **ROSA:** Hon, everything's upside-down your first week. *(she flips it)* You got somewhere to be?

> **YOU:** I have a dream and $87. So, no.

> **ROSA:** *(laughs, genuine)* Okay. Okay, I like you. Listen — I need three things from the back, and I pay in breakfast. Deal?

The delivery tutorial: three items inside the bodega, each with a **fun-fact sticker**:
- **The Mangos** — *"Bodegas: NYC's cornerstones. There are over 10,000. You now know the most important one."*
- **The Coffee Can** — *"A 'regular coffee' means milk and two sugars. Nobody knows why. It's just true."*
- **The Bread** — *"Bread arrives before sunrise, every day, rain or shine. The city eats early."*

> **ROSA:** Not bad. You got hands, you got eyes. *(she looks at you a second too long, deciding something)* …I know an empty corner. Owner owes me. You want it?

> **YOU:** For what?

> **ROSA:** For whatever you're gonna be. Everyone's got one here — a thing. You'll figure out yours. C'mon. But first — *(she ties an apron on you, way too fast to stop her)* — the morning rush doesn't care about your dreams. Let's go.

> **Design note:** Rosa performs the game's thesis in one scene: the city is strict but kind, and kindness arrives as *a job*. The apron-tying is the first tactile moment — controllers buzz once, softly.

---

## 5:00–10:00 — THE FIRST RUSH

Five customers, five forgiving assembly mini-games. Each order is a micro-lesson in NYC ordering culture:

1. **"Regular coffee."** *(Pour, two sugars, milk — the game gently highlights each step; a wrong step just winks at you.)*
   - Customer: "You're new. Rosa trains 'em right though. You'll do fine."
2. **"Bacon egg and cheese. Salt, pepper, ketchup."**
   - On-screen card: *"That whole sentence is a complete order. You speak New York now."*
3. **"Everything bagel, scallion cream cheese, toasted."** *(The bagel is genuinely enormous. This is a joke the game makes once and never explains.)*
4. **"Chopped cheese on a hero, letta, tomahta, hots."** *(Slang card: *hero* = a big sub roll. You do not ask about the hots.)*
5. **A quiet older man. Just: "The usual, please."**
   - Rosa, quietly: "That's Mr. Idris. Two sugars. Every day for nine years. You'll see him tomorrow."
   - *(This line is doing triple duty: teaching 'regulars,' planting Idris for your own cart, and establishing the game's deepest comfort — people come back.)*

The rush ends. Golden light slants through the bodega window. Rosa slides a plate over: bacon, egg, and cheese — the one you made.

> **ROSA:** Eat. Then I got a delivery. My friend works right in the middle of everything, and I promised her a coffee since Tuesday. You take it. *(beat)* Trust me. You want to see this.

> **Design note:** The starter trinity of NYC ordering is learned through *doing* in under five minutes. Slang cards appear only for glowing words the player can ignore — teaching without testing.

---

## 10:00–15:00 — THE WOW WONK *(wonder beat #1)*

A short, guided walk. The game teaches one thing: crowds. **The Shuffle** — a gentle mini-game about walking through people without stopping (drift toward gaps; brushing someone just makes them say "scuse me," which is a *correct* interaction, not a failure).

You descend into a subway entrance — Priya, the conductor, waves you through ("Go on. It's one stop. You're with me.") — and up a stairwell into—

**Times Square. Golden hour. The screens all turn at once — a designed moment where every billboard syncs to warm light, just for you.**

Music swells. The camera does the thing. **Postcard Mode** triggers automatically — letterbox, soft zoom, title:

<div align="center"><b>📌 Your first postcard.</b><br><i>"Times Square — 300,000 people cross here every day. Today, so did you."</i></div>

The coffee is delivered to Rosa's friend, a theater usher, who looks at you exactly like Rosa did.

> **USHER:** First day? *(the player nods)* Come back when you're somebody. They all come back when they're somebody.

Delivery complete. On the way back, a street performer plays; the game slows time for four seconds and lets you just… listen. No objective. No prompt. Just the city.

> **Design note:** The "Come back when you're somebody" line plants a decade-long ambition in one sentence — the player's quiet promise to the city. The four seconds of unpressured music is the first *mood* beat: the game will sometimes just let you exist in New York. That permission is the luxury the whole game sells.

---

## 15:00–20:00 — SUBWAY UNIVERSITY

Priya walks you to the platform.

> **PRIYA:** Rules of the road. Trains go two ways: uptown and downtown. Uptown's where you came from. Downtown's where everything new happens. You'll have opinions about this by winter.

> **YOU:** What if I take the wrong one?

> **PRIYA:** *(smiles)* Then you get out, look around, and get on the other one. This isn't a test, kid. It's a subway.

One guided ride. Windows flicker with tunnel light. Priya draws two lines on the window in two swipes of her glove — "one goes under the river, one stays with the lights." **Map copies them badly.** She tells Map that's fine. Then she taps Map's crooked copy: "See? You already can read it." — and the doors open on a **busker** playing saxophone under the tile echo. A small crowd sways. You drop a coin *(your first kindness — the "I Got You" system's invisible counter starts here, unmarked)*.

Exiting one stop early "just to see" seeds **Happy Accident #1**: a hidden record shop, closed, with a hand-written sign: *"Back soon. The city knows when."* (It matters at Week 3. Everything matters at Week 3.)

> **Design note:** The subway — the #1 fear of would-be visitors — is converted from intimidating to *cozy* in five minutes flat, and "getting lost" is pre-reframed as discovery before the player can ever experience it as failure.

---

## 20:00–26:00 — YOUR CORNER

Rosa walks you to the empty corner. A fire hydrant, a pigeon with attitude, a rectangle of afternoon sun. She hands you a folded cart.

> **ROSA:** It's ugly now. So was mine. *(beat)* It's yours. What're you calling it?

**You name your cart.** The name immediately appears on the awning, and — the game tells you, without saying it — on every receipt, every postcard, every piece of your story from now on.

First solo rush: three customers. The mini-games run faster now, and the game *lets you feel the speed* — you are, briefly, actually good at this.

Customer one: a woman with a stroller who says "oh, finally, SOMETHING on this corner."
Customer two: a teenager who asks for "whatever's cold."
Customer three: **Mr. Idris.** Two sugars. He looks at the cart, looks at you, nods once.

> **IDRIS:** Rosa said you'd be here. *(he takes the cup)* She's usually right. It's annoying.

> **REGULAR UNLOCKED — Mr. Idris.** *"Two sugars. Every day. You'll see him tomorrow."*

Rosa appears at your shoulder, counts your register with you: **$87 → $112.**

> **ROSA:** Twenty-five dollars on your first day. *(beat, quieter)* Nineteen years ago I made eleven. *(she pats the cart twice — the same way she patted the mangoes)* Lock up tight. The city takes care of itself at night. That part took me a while to learn.

> **Design note:** Ownership moment — naming is the single most powerful identity mechanic in mobile games, and it happens at minute ~22, right after competence is established. Idris's unlock converts a stranger into a *promise*.

---

## 26:00–30:00 — THE FIRST POSTCARD *(wonder beat #2)*

The sky goes amber, then plum. You climb the fire escape of the building next to your corner (the game teaches climbing here, casually) with a coffee. The city below: lights coming on street by street, steam from a manhole, a taxi's roof gliding past.

**The day composes itself into a postcard, live, in your hands:**

<div align="center">

📌 **DAY 1 — [Your Cart's Name]**
*$87 → $112.*
*Met: Rosa. Idris. Learned: "regular" means small. Learned: "on line," not "in line."*
*The regulars are waiting.*

</div>

A soft glow around the share button. Never a prompt. Never a nudge. Just an option, glowing, like a streetlight.

Then — across the skyline, one dark window flickers. Flickers again. Then holds, gold.

> **ROSA (quieter, following your eyes):** That one's the kid who ordered "whatever's cold." *(beat)* You were their first warm thing today.

> **ROSA (from below, calling up):** Lights out, rookie! Big day tomorrow. They're gonna want the usual.

Fade to black over the skyline. One last sound: the key jingle — keys turning in your new lock.

**Day 2 loads with the whole city opened up.**

> **Design note:** The session ends on beauty, ownership, and an open loop. The postcard ritual — installed in 30 minutes, repeated for the life of the game — is the retention engine and the UGC engine running off the same flywheel.
